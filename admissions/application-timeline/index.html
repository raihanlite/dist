Study Abroad Application Timeline from Bangladesh — Month by Month

19 Nov 2026 · 7 min read · Last checked 19 Nov 2026

Studying abroad takes about a year of planning. Students who start 12–14 months before their course begins have time to prepare for tests, apply for scholarships, wait for offers and get a visa without panic. Students who start 3 months before usually miss the scholarship deadlines and end up paying full fees — or waiting another year. This guide gives you a month-by-month plan for the two main intakes: September (autumn) and January (spring).

September intake: month by month

This plan assumes you want to start in September 2027. Shift the months if your target year is different.

WhenWhat to do
June – July 2026 (14 months before)Choose your countries and level. Set a budget using the cost estimator. Shortlist 6–8 universities. Check which scholarships you are eligible for and note their deadlines — most fall between August and December.
August 2026Apply for scholarships that open now (Chevening opens in August). Start IELTS/TOEFL preparation. Request transcripts and certificates from your school or university.
September – October 2026Submit scholarship applications (Chevening closes early October; DAAD EPOS courses close August–October; Commonwealth main round closes in October). Take your English test. Ask teachers or employers for recommendation letters — give them at least 3 weeks.
November – December 2026Write your statement of purpose. Submit university applications for early deadlines (some UK and US universities close in December–January). Commonwealth Shared Scholarships open in November and close 8 December.
January – February 2027Finish university applications. Erasmus Mundus programmes close between October and January — check each programme's date. Prepare for scholarship interviews (Chevening shortlists run October–February).
March – April 2027Attend interviews (Chevening interviews run March–April). MEXT embassy applications open in April. Australia Awards results come through. Compare offers as they arrive.
May – June 2027Accept your best offer and pay any deposit. Scholarship results arrive (Chevening from mid-June). Arrange accommodation and health insurance. Gather visa documents.
July 2027Apply for your visa as soon as you have your offer and financial proof. Chevening's unconditional-offer deadline is 8 July. Do not book non-refundable flights yet.
August 2027Visa decision, pre-departure briefing, flights, packing. Confirm your accommodation and arrival arrangements.
September 2027Arrive, register at your university, open a bank account, and start your course.

January intake: month by month

January starts are offered by fewer universities and fewer scholarships — many fully funded scholarships only run for autumn starts. Check whether your target course and scholarship actually offer a January start before you plan around it.

WhenWhat to do
October – November 2025 (14 months before)Choose countries and shortlist universities that offer January starts. Check scholarship availability — most government scholarships will not be open for this intake.
December 2025 – January 2026Prepare for IELTS/TOEFL. Request transcripts and recommendation letters.
February – April 2026Take your English test. Write your statement of purpose. Submit university applications.
May – July 2026Compare offers, accept one, pay the deposit. Arrange accommodation and insurance.
August – September 2026Apply for your visa. Allow extra time — embassies are busy with September-intake applicants.
October – November 2026Visa decision, flights, pre-departure preparation.
January 2027Arrive and start your course.

The deadlines that catch people out

  • Scholarships close before university applications. Chevening, DAAD EPOS and Commonwealth all close months before most university deadlines. If you plan your timeline around university deadlines, you miss the funding.
  • Recommendation letters take weeks. Teachers and employers are busy. Ask at least 3 weeks before you need the letter, and give them your CV and a note about what the letter should cover.
  • Transcripts and attestation take time. Getting transcripts from your board or university, and getting documents attested, can take weeks in Bangladesh. Start early.
  • English test results take days, retakes take weeks. IELTS results come in 1–5 days, TOEFL in about 3 days — but booking a retake, sitting it and getting the new result takes a month. Leave a retake buffer before your earliest deadline.
  • Visas are the bottleneck. Allow 6–12 weeks for a visa after you have your offer and financial documents. German student visas and US visa interviews can take longer at peak times. Never book a non-refundable flight before your visa is approved.

If you miss a deadline

It happens — a scholarship closes, a test score arrives late, a university rejects you. Missing one deadline is not the end of your plan, but it does change it. First, check whether the same scholarship or programme has another round: many scholarships run every year, so a missed Chevening or MEXT deadline means preparing properly for the next cycle, not giving up. Second, look at the January intake — fewer courses, but a real option if your target university offers it. Third, use the extra months well: improve your English score, gain work experience, or strengthen your SOP — all of these make your next application stronger than the one you missed. What you should not do is pay an agent who promises a "late admission" or a "guaranteed" place. Universities and scholarship bodies do not accept late applications, and anyone who says otherwise is selling you something that does not exist.

Working while you wait

The months between applying and departing are not dead time. If you are a graduate, a relevant job — even a short one — strengthens your profile for scholarships that value work experience, and it gives you savings for flights, deposits and the first month abroad. If you are still studying, use the time to finish your degree strongly, since your final grades matter for unconditional offers. You can also use the wait to learn the language of your destination country: basic German, Japanese or Chinese makes daily life far easier on arrival and improves your job prospects after graduation. And start the practical preparations early — passport renewal, document attestation, and health checks all take longer than you expect in Bangladesh, and doing them under deadline pressure is how mistakes happen.

Your application checklist

  • Passport valid for at least 6 months beyond your planned stay (renew early — a new passport takes weeks)
  • Academic transcripts and certificates, attested
  • English test result (IELTS / TOEFL / PTE)
  • Statement of purpose, tailored to each university
  • Two recommendation letters
  • CV
  • University offer letter
  • Financial proof (bank statement, blocked account, GIC, or scholarship award letter)
  • Health insurance, where required
  • Visa application, fee receipt and photos

One timeline, many deadlines: every university and every scholarship sets its own dates. This plan is a framework, not a substitute for the official deadlines. Make your own calendar with the exact dates for your shortlisted universities and scholarships, and check it every week. A single missed deadline can cost you a year.
