GPA Conversion for Bangladeshi Students — SSC, HSC and CGPA Explained

19 Nov 2026 · 7 min read · Last checked 19 Nov 2026

Bangladesh uses two grading scales that confuse almost every applicant: SSC and HSC are graded out of 5.0, while most universities grade out of 4.0. On top of that, universities in the USA, UK and Germany each read your grades differently. This guide explains how your SSC, HSC and CGPA translate, what universities actually do with your grades, and why you should never convert your GPA yourself on an application form unless the university asks you to.

Key facts

The two Bangladeshi scales

SSC and HSC results are reported as a GPA out of 5.0, calculated from your subject grade points. A+ is 5.0, A is 4.0, A− is 3.5, B is 3.0, C is 2.0, D is 1.0, and F is 0. Your GPA is the average of your subject grade points (with the 4th subject bonus applied under the board's rules).

At university level, most Bangladeshi universities use a 4.0 CGPA scale, where A+ is 4.0, A is 3.75, A− is 3.5, B+ is 3.25, B is 3.0, B− is 2.75, C+ is 2.5, C is 2.25, D is 2.0, and F is 0. But scales differ between universities — some use different grade points, and some older transcripts use different systems entirely. Always go by what is printed on your own transcript, not by a general table.

SSC / HSC GPA (out of 5.0) to the 4.0 scale

There is no single official conversion formula. Universities and credential evaluators each do it their own way. The table below is a rough guide used by many advisers — it is an approximation, not an official conversion, and you should never present it as your official GPA.

A simple proportional conversion — (your GPA ÷ 5) × 4 — gives a similar result (for example, 4.5 ÷ 5 × 4 = 3.6), and some universities use exactly that. But others use their own tables, and some evaluate your marks subject by subject instead. The honest answer is: the university decides, not you.

CGPA (out of 4.0) — what universities expect

If your Bangladeshi university already uses a 4.0 scale, your CGPA is directly comparable to the US 4.0 scale. Most universities abroad ask for a minimum CGPA, and the table below shows the typical thresholds. These are common requirements, not universal rules — always check the programme's own page.

CGPA (out of 4.0)What it usually means
3.70 – 4.00Excellent — competitive for top universities and most scholarships
3.30 – 3.69Very good — meets the minimum for most competitive programmes
3.00 – 3.29Good — meets the minimum for most master's programmes abroad
2.50 – 2.99Below the minimum for many programmes; some universities accept it with strong test scores or work experience
Below 2.50Very few programmes abroad will accept this without significant other strengths

UK degree classes: what they mean for Bangladeshis

UK universities state their requirements as degree classes, not GPAs. For a Bangladeshi bachelor's degree, UK universities commonly treat the following as roughly equivalent — but each university publishes its own conversion, and it varies:

UK degree classTypical Bangladeshi CGPA equivalent (out of 4.0)Typical percentage
First class3.5 – 4.070%+
Upper second (2:1)3.0 – 3.360–69%
Lower second (2:2)2.7 – 2.950–59%

Most UK master's programmes ask for a 2:1. That is why a CGPA of 3.0 is such an important threshold for Bangladeshi applicants — it is the line most UK universities draw. Some competitive programmes ask for a First (around 3.5). Commonwealth Scholarships require a 2:1, or a 2:2 plus a relevant postgraduate qualification. If your CGPA is below 3.0, your options narrow — but they do not disappear: work experience, a strong SOP and a good English score can help, and some universities accept lower grades for less competitive programmes.

Germany: the Bavarian formula

German universities convert foreign grades with a published formula — the modified Bavarian formula — which you can use yourself to estimate your German grade:

German grade = 1 + 3 × (best possible grade − your grade) ÷ (best possible grade − lowest passing grade)

For a Bangladeshi CGPA out of 4.0, the best possible grade is 4.0 and the lowest passing grade is usually 2.0. So a CGPA of 3.5 becomes 1 + 3 × (4.0 − 3.5) ÷ (4.0 − 2.0) = 1.75. German grades run from 1.0 (best) to 4.0 (pass) — so a lower number is better, the opposite of Bangladesh. Most German master's programmes ask for a German grade of 2.5 or better, which is roughly a CGPA of 3.0. Some competitive programmes ask for 2.0 or better. Always check the programme's own requirement, and note that some universities use a different lowest passing grade in the formula.

Percentage: how to calculate it

Some universities ask for your marks as a percentage. The calculation is straightforward: (marks you obtained ÷ total marks) × 100. For HSC, your total is the sum of all subject totals. If your board or university already prints a percentage on your transcript, use that — do not recalculate it yourself.

Credential evaluation (WES and others)

Some universities — especially in the USA and Canada — ask for a credential evaluation from an organisation such as WES (World Education Services). WES reads your original transcript and produces a US-equivalent GPA. You do not convert anything yourself; you send WES your documents and they do it. A WES evaluation costs money and takes weeks, so only get one if a university on your shortlist requires it. Most universities in the UK, Germany, Australia and Japan do their own evaluation and do not ask for WES.

What to do in practice

  • Enter your GPA exactly as printed on your transcript, on its original scale
  • Always send the full transcript with subject-wise marks — universities evaluate the original, not your conversion
  • Check each programme's minimum CGPA or degree class before you apply
  • Remember the key thresholds: CGPA 3.0 for most master's programmes and UK 2:1; around 3.5 for First-class and the most competitive programmes
  • Only get a WES evaluation if a university on your shortlist requires it
  • Use the StudyKoro calculators to work out your GPA under the official Bangladeshi rules

Do not trust conversion websites: many websites offer to "convert" your GPA for a fee, or publish tables that claim to be official. There is no universal official conversion table for Bangladeshi grades. The university you apply to is the only authority on how it reads your grades — and most of them publish their requirements on their own admissions pages.
