IELTS vs TOEFL for Bangladeshi Students — Which Test to Take

19 Nov 2026 · 8 min read · Last checked 19 Nov 2026

Almost every university abroad asks for proof of English. For Bangladeshi students, the choice is almost always IELTS or TOEFL. Both are accepted by thousands of universities, both are valid for two years, and both cost real money — so choosing the wrong one wastes time and a test fee. This guide compares them properly, including the big change TOEFL made in January 2026, and the fees as they stand in Bangladesh.

What changed in 2026

Two changes matter for anyone choosing a test now.

TOEFL was redesigned on 21 January 2026. The test is now about 90 minutes instead of 2 hours, and scores are reported on a new 1–6 band scale instead of 0–120. During the 2026–2028 transition, your score report shows both the new band and a comparable score on the old 0–120 scale, so universities can read whichever one they publish. But not every university has switched: some publish requirements on the old scale, some on the new one — and a few have stopped accepting TOEFL altogether for tests taken after 21 January 2026. Oxford is one example. Always check your target university's own English requirements page, and check which TOEFL scale it quotes.

IELTS in Bangladesh is now computer-delivered. Paper-based IELTS has been withdrawn in Bangladesh — the last paper-based sessions ran in early 2026 — so you will sit the test on a computer at a British Council or IDP centre. The test itself is the same; only the delivery changed. IELTS for UKVI is also computer-only now.

The benchmark most Bangladeshi applicants need: IELTS 6.5 overall (usually with no band below 6.0) or the TOEFL equivalent, for most postgraduate programmes. Undergraduate programmes usually ask for 6.0–6.5. Top universities ask for 7.0–7.5. Medicine, law and teaching often ask for 7.0–7.5 with minimums in every section.

The rule is simple: the university's requirement decides, not the country. If your shortlist is all US universities, take TOEFL. If it is all UK, take IELTS — and take IELTS for UKVI if you will need a UK student visa, because the visa route has its own approved-test list. If your shortlist mixes countries, check each university's page and pick the test that covers the most of them.

UK visa warning: for a UK student visa, the safe choice is IELTS for UKVI. TOEFL's status on the Home Office's approved-test list has changed over time, so do not book TOEFL for a UK visa without checking the current GOV.UK list of approved Secure English Language Tests first.

Format: what the test actually feels like

IELTS Academic has four sections: Listening (30 minutes), Reading (60 minutes), Writing (60 minutes, two tasks) and Speaking (11–14 minutes, face to face with an examiner). Listening, Reading and Writing are taken on a computer; Speaking is a real conversation with a person, usually on the same day or within a few days. Writing and Speaking are scored by human examiners.

TOEFL iBT is fully computer-based, including Speaking, where you record answers to prompts. Reading and Listening are now adaptive — the test adjusts difficulty as you answer. All four sections are scored by ETS's AI system, trained on human ratings. The whole test takes about 90 minutes. TOEFL also offers MyBest scores, which combine your best section scores across multiple sittings — about 1,000 universities accept them.

Which is easier depends on you: students who are comfortable speaking to a person often prefer IELTS Speaking; students who type fast and prefer a fully computerised test often prefer TOEFL. Students who struggle with handwriting do well on both now that IELTS is computer-delivered in Bangladesh.

Fees in Bangladesh — and why they move

IELTS fees in Bangladesh are pegged to the US dollar and have changed several times in 2026. The most recent figures reported in November 2026 are ৳31,100 for IELTS Academic or General Training on computer, ৳34,850 for IELTS for UKVI, and ৳18,600 for a One Skill Retake. Earlier in 2026 the same test cost ৳24,950 — so do not budget from an old figure. Confirm the current fee on the British Council or IDP booking page before you pay.

TOEFL iBT costs US$215 in Bangladesh, plus taxes, paid by international card through your ETS account. That makes TOEFL roughly the same price as IELTS at current exchange rates — the fee should not decide your choice. A retake costs the full fee again on either test, so budget for a possible second sitting.

Preparation tips

  • Take a full mock test before you book. Both IELTS and TOEFL have free official practice tests. Your mock score tells you which test suits you and how much preparation you need.
  • Prepare for the format, not just the English. IELTS Writing Task 1 (describing a chart) and TOEFL's integrated tasks (reading + listening, then writing or speaking) are skills you can practise — they are not the same as general English.
  • Give yourself 6–8 weeks. Most students need 6–8 weeks of focused preparation to lift their score by half a band. A week of cramming rarely moves a score.
  • Book early. Test centres in Dhaka fill up, especially before university deadlines. Book at least a month ahead, and leave time for a retake before your application deadline.
  • Check section minimums. Many universities require a minimum in each section (for example, no band below 6.0), not just an overall score. A 7.0 overall with a 5.5 in Writing can still be rejected.

Checklist before you book

  • Check your shortlisted universities' pages — which test and which score does each one require?
  • If the UK is on your list, check whether you need IELTS for UKVI for the visa
  • Take a free official mock test for both IELTS and TOEFL before choosing
  • Book at least a month ahead, and leave time for a retake before your earliest deadline
  • Confirm the current fee on the provider's booking page before you pay
  • Check section minimums, not just the overall score
