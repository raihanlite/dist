Ausbildung in Germany from Bangladesh

22 Nov 2026 · 11 min read · Last checked 22 Nov 2026

An Ausbildung is Germany's paid vocational training: you work in a company, attend a vocational school, and earn a monthly salary from your first month. You do not need a university degree, and there is no tuition. For a Bangladeshi student with HSC and German at B1 level, it is one of the most realistic routes into Germany, and into a skilled job afterwards. More than 270,000 training places went unfilled in 2024, according to the Federal Employment Agency, so German employers actively recruit from abroad.

This guide covers what an Ausbildung is, what it pays, the German you need, the visa, and how to apply from Bangladesh. If you want a university degree instead, read our guide to studying in Germany.

What is an Ausbildung?

An Ausbildung is a recognised vocational qualification, not a degree. Germany has more than 320 recognised training occupations, listed by the Federal Institute for Vocational Education and Training (BIBB): nursing, IT, mechatronics, hospitality, logistics, crafts, office work and retail, among others.

Most training is dual: you spend most of the week working in a company and one or two days at a vocational school. The company pays you a monthly training salary, and the school is free. At the end, you sit an exam before the responsible chamber (IHK for industry and commerce, HWK for crafts) and receive a recognised certificate.

The other form is school-based training: it takes place mainly at a vocational school, with practical placements. It is common in health, care and some social professions. Pay is not guaranteed in school-based training, and some private schools charge fees, so check the exact terms before you accept a place.

The key difference from university: you earn from the first month, and you finish with a qualification that German employers know and hire for. The trade-off is that the training is in German, and your career stays in that occupation unless you retrain.

Who can apply from Bangladesh

You do not need a university degree. There is no single school qualification that German law demands for dual training: employers decide whom they hire. In practice, HSC (12 years of schooling) is the usual level for Bangladeshi applicants, and a diploma, such as a Diploma in Engineering, opens more professions. Check how your certificate is rated in the anabin database, and be ready to get a statement from the Central Office for Foreign Education (ZAB) if the embassy asks for one.

There is no legal maximum age for a dual Ausbildung. The under-35 age limit you may read about applies only to the separate visa for seeking a training place, not to the visa with a signed contract. Older applicants can still succeed, but the embassy may ask more questions about your motivation and career plans.

What you must have before the visa is a signed training contract (Ausbildungsvertrag) with a German employer, or admission to a state-recognised school-based programme. The contract is the entry ticket: without it, there is no training visa.

What you will earn

Germany sets a legal minimum training allowance (Mindestausbildungsvergütung). For training that starts in 2026, the Federal Institute for Vocational Education and Training (BIBB) gives these gross monthly minimums:

Training yearMinimum gross pay per month
1st year€724 (about ৳99,000)
2nd year€854 (about ৳1,17,000)
3rd year€977 (about ৳1,34,000)
4th year€1,014 (about ৳1,39,000)

The starting year's rates apply for your whole training. For training that starts in 2027, the first-year minimum rises to €764 a month (about ৳1,05,000).

The minimum is a floor, not the typical pay. The average training allowance across all sectors was about €1,070 a month in 2025 (BIBB), and trainees under collective agreements averaged €1,209 a month (about ৳1,66,000). Industrial companies, banks and the public sector usually pay well above the minimum because they follow collective agreements.

Nursing is one of the best-paid fields: trainees under public-sector collective agreements commonly earn around €1,100 a month or more in the first year, rising over the three years. Hospitality and retail are usually closer to the legal minimum.

Pay is gross: tax and social insurance come off before you are paid, so your bank transfer is lower than the figure in your contract. Your contract states the exact amount, so never accept a verbal figure. You also get paid annual leave and the same worker protections as other employees.

The German you need

Training and vocational school run in German, so the language is the real gatekeeper. The federal government's portal, Make it in Germany, says B1 (CEFR) is normally required for qualified vocational training. The German Embassy Dhaka's own checklist says usually at least A2 is enough, except medical professions, which need at least B1. In practice, most employers ask for B1 or B2, so treat B1 as the safe minimum and aim higher if you can.

Nursing and healthcare are stricter: B2 is expected in practice, because you must understand medical instructions and speak with patients. Some technical trades will consider A2 if you commit to reaching B1 during the training, but do not count on it.

Take a recognised exam: Goethe-Institut, telc or ÖSD certificates are accepted. The Goethe-Institut in Dhaka runs courses and exams, and it is the natural place to prepare. Start early: reaching B1 from zero takes many months of steady study, and the language is usually the longest part of the whole plan.

Your residence permit also lets you attend a preparatory German course in Germany alongside the training, and during the training you may take a second job of up to 20 hours a week, if it is separate from your training.

The visa

Bangladeshi citizens need a national visa for vocational training under Section 16a of the Residence Act (Aufenthaltsgesetz). You apply at the German Embassy in Dhaka after you sign the contract. The training visa is different from the German student visa: it is for vocational training, not university study, and it has its own financial-proof rules.

Proof of funds

Your situationWhat the embassy needs (2026)
Company-based (dual) trainingTraining pay of at least €1,048 gross (€822 net) a month (about ৳1,44,000 / ৳1,13,000). If your pay is lower, top up the difference with a blocked account or a declaration of commitment.
School-based trainingAt least €959 net a month (about ৳1,31,000), shown by a blocked account, a declaration of commitment, or a scholarship.

If you get free housing, the required amount is €380 a month lower. A declaration of commitment (Verpflichtungserklärung) is a formal promise by a person living in Germany to cover your living costs.

How the visa works

The Federal Employment Agency checks your contract: your pay and working conditions must match those of German trainees. This happens inside the visa process, and you do not apply for it yourself. Your employer can get a pre-approval from the agency before you apply, which speeds up the visa considerably.

The visa fee is €75 (about ৳10,300), paid at the embassy. Processing takes at least four weeks once your file is complete, according to the German Embassy Dhaka, and appointments can take weeks to get, so apply early and only with complete documents: incomplete files are not accepted.

No training place yet?

If you do not have a contract, there is a separate visa for seeking a vocational training place under Section 17. It needs a school certificate that gives access to higher education, German at B1, and a blocked account of €1,091 a month (about ৳1,49,000). It lasts up to nine months, only for applicants under 35, and you may work up to 20 hours a week and do up to two weeks of trial work while you search.

How to apply, step by step

  1. Learn German to B1 (B2 for nursing). Take the exam at the Goethe-Institut in Dhaka.
  2. Prepare your documents: a German CV (Lebenslauf), a motivation letter, and your SSC, HSC or diploma certificates with certified German translations. Check your certificates in the anabin database.
  3. Find training places on the Federal Employment Agency's job search, on ausbildung.de, and on company websites. Apply to many places: interviews are usually by video call from Bangladesh.
  4. Sign the training contract (Ausbildungsvertrag). Check the pay, the start date and the occupation in writing before you sign.
  5. Apply for the visa at the German Embassy in Dhaka with the contract, the training plan, your language certificate, and proof of funds if your pay is below the threshold.
  6. Fly and start. Most training places start in August or September, so start the whole process 12 to 18 months ahead.

What it costs before you earn: the German course and exam, certified translations, the visa fee of €75, the flight, and your first month in Germany until the first salary arrives. There is no tuition to pay. Use the cost estimator to plan your first months, and see how Germany compares with other countries in the cost comparison.

Nursing is the most common choice for Bangladeshi applicants, and it is one of the best-paid fields. Hospitality and logistics take the most beginners, because the German requirement is lower and employers recruit widely. IT and mechatronics pay well but expect stronger German and, often, some prior knowledge.

After the Ausbildung

After you complete the training, you can stay in Germany for up to 18 months to look for qualified work, with a residence permit for job-seeking under Section 20(1) No. 3 of the Residence Act. Apply for it at the local foreigners authority before your training permit expires. During those 18 months you may take up any job while you search for a qualified one.

Once you have a qualified job offer, you switch to a residence permit for skilled work without leaving Germany. And after the training plus two years of work in Germany, you can apply for a settlement permit under Section 18c(1), if you meet the additional requirements for it. Learning German is therefore not just useful for daily life: it shortens the path to staying long term.

Never pay for a training place. A dual Ausbildung pays you: the company pays your salary and the vocational school is free. Anyone asking for a large fee for a "guaranteed" training place or a "guaranteed" visa is selling you something that does not exist. No agent can guarantee a visa: the German Embassy in Dhaka decides, and the Federal Employment Agency checks your contract. Verify every offer against the official sources below before you pay anything.

Documents checklist

  • Valid passport (issued within the last 10 years, with at least two empty pages)
  • Signed training contract (Ausbildungsvertrag) and training plan (Ausbildungsplan)
  • Pre-approval from the Federal Employment Agency (not mandatory, but it speeds up the visa)
  • SSC, HSC or diploma certificates, with certified German translations
  • German language certificate (B1; B2 for nursing and healthcare)
  • Motivation letter and CV (Lebenslauf) in German
  • Proof of funds if your training pay is below the threshold (blocked account or declaration of commitment)
  • Health insurance proof
  • Visa application form, biometric photos and the €75 fee
  • Anything else the embassy's current checklist asks for
