Study in Canada from Bangladesh

19 Nov 2026 · 13 min read · Last checked 19 Nov 2026

Canada is one of the most popular study destinations for Bangladeshi students. It offers good universities, the chance to work while you study, and a clear work permit after graduation. It is not cheap, and the rules have tightened since 2024 — there is now a cap on study permits, a provincial attestation letter, and stricter language rules for the post-graduation work permit. This guide explains the costs, the requirements and the steps in order, with figures checked at official Canadian sources.

BDT figures in this guide use about ৳87 per CAD $1 (Wise mid-market rate, 18 November 2026). Exchange rates move daily, so treat every BDT figure as a rough guide. The Canadian dollar figure is the one that matters.

Why study in Canada

Canada has about 100 public universities and many public colleges, and its degrees are recognised around the world. Popular subjects for Bangladeshi students include computer science, engineering, business, health sciences and data analytics. Most teaching is in English; in Quebec, French is the main language.

Three things make Canada attractive. First, you can work part time while you study, which helps with living costs. Second, the post-graduation work permit lets you work in Canada for up to 3 years after you finish, with no job offer needed. Third, Canadian work experience can help if you later apply for permanent residence through an economic immigration programme. None of this is guaranteed — the work permit has its own rules, and permanent residence is a separate application.

There are real downsides too. Tuition for international students is high, winters are cold in most provinces, and housing in Toronto and Vancouver is expensive and hard to find. The study permit cap also means more competition for places: apply early, and have a back-up university in mind.

Tuition and living costs

Tuition is set by each university and college, so the exact fee is always on the institution's own website. National averages from EduCanada (Statistics Canada, 2026) give a useful starting point:

Business, engineering, medicine and other specialist programmes usually cost more than the average. Books, lab fees and health insurance are normally extra. EduCanada advises budgeting a minimum of CAD $23,000 a year for living costs (about ৳20 lakh), and more if family members come with you. Rent is the biggest living cost, and it varies a lot: a shared room in a smaller city such as Halifax or Winnipeg costs far less than a room in Toronto or Vancouver.

Admission requirements

Each university sets its own requirements, so check the programme page of every university you apply to. In general, Bangladeshi applicants need:

  • For a bachelor's degree: HSC (Higher Secondary Certificate) with good grades, usually in subjects related to the programme. Some universities ask for specific HSC subjects, for example mathematics and physics for engineering.
  • For a master's degree: a 4-year bachelor's degree (or a 3-year degree plus a master's, depending on the university) with a good CGPA, usually 3.0 or above on a 4.0 scale. Some programmes ask for a GRE score, work experience or a portfolio.
  • For a PhD: a master's degree in a related field, a research proposal, and usually contact with a supervisor before you apply.
  • English test: IELTS Academic is the most widely accepted test. Universities usually ask for 6.0–6.5 overall for undergraduate study and 6.5–7.0 for postgraduate study, with no band below 6.0. TOEFL, PTE and Duolingo are accepted by some universities — check each one.
  • Other documents: academic transcripts and certificates, a statement of purpose, and one or two recommendation letters. Some programmes ask for a CV or an interview.

Most universities have two main intakes: September (fall) and January (winter). September has the most places and scholarships. Apply 8–12 months before the course starts. You apply directly on each university's website, and most charge an application fee of about CAD $100–$150.

Before you accept an offer, check that the school is a designated learning institution (DLI). Only DLIs can host international students, and only some DLI programmes qualify for the post-graduation work permit. The DLI list is on the IRCC website.

Scholarships

Full scholarships for undergraduate study in Canada are rare, and competition is strong. Most Bangladeshi students pay full tuition. Still, check these options before you apply — deadlines fall months before the course starts.

Also ask each university about graduate assistantships and teaching assistantships. For master's and PhD students, an assistantship can cover a large part of tuition and pay a monthly stipend — but it is a job, not a scholarship, and it depends on the department's funding.

Be careful: no genuine scholarship asks you to pay a fee to apply, and no agent can guarantee you a scholarship or a visa. If someone promises a "100% scholarship" or a "guaranteed visa" for a payment, walk away.

Student visa (study permit)

Bangladeshi citizens need a study permit to study in Canada for more than 6 months. You apply online to Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) after you have a letter of acceptance from a DLI. Apply as early as you can — processing from Bangladesh can take several weeks, and you also need time for biometrics.

Fees

The study permit fee is CAD $150 per person (about ৳13,000), and biometrics (fingerprints and photo) cost CAD $85 per person (about ৳7,400). You give biometrics at a visa application centre after you apply. A visitor visa is issued automatically with an approved study permit at no extra charge.

Financial proof

You must show you can pay, without working in Canada, for your first year of tuition, your living expenses, and travel to and from Canada. For applications made on or after 1 September 2026, the living-cost amount for a single applicant outside Quebec is CAD $23,448 a year (about ৳20.4 lakh) — up from CAD $22,895 before that date. Quebec sets its own amounts. The amount does not include tuition or travel.

Common ways to show the money are a guaranteed investment certificate (GIC) from a participating Canadian bank, bank statements for the past 6 months showing the source of the funds, proof of a student or education loan from a bank, proof that you paid the first year of tuition and housing, or a scholarship letter. You must also explain how you will pay for the rest of the programme if it lasts more than a year.

Provincial attestation letter (PAL)

Most study permit applicants need a provincial attestation letter (PAL), or a territorial attestation letter, from the province where they will study. Your DLI tells you how to get one — do not apply for a PAL yourself before the school instructs you. Two changes matter for 2026: master's and doctoral students at public DLIs do not need a PAL from 1 January 2026, and Canada expects to issue up to 408,000 study permits in 2026, including 155,000 for newly arriving students. A PAL is linked to one school and one cap year, so if you change schools you will usually need a new one.

What IRCC checks

Besides money and the PAL, IRCC checks that your letter of acceptance is genuine, that you are a genuine student, that you will leave Canada at the end of your stay if required, and that you meet health and character requirements. Some applicants are asked for a medical exam or an interview. Keep every document honest and consistent — a false document can lead to a refusal and a ban from applying again.

Working while studying

If you are a full-time student at a DLI and your study permit allows it, you can work off campus without a separate work permit — up to 24 hours a week while classes are in session, and full time during scheduled breaks such as the summer and winter holidays. The 24-hour limit has applied since 8 November 2024; working more than the limit is a breach of your permit conditions and can cost you your student status. You need a Social Insurance Number (SIN) before you start work, and you cannot start working before your programme starts.

You can also work on campus without a work permit if you are a full-time student at a DLI. On-campus jobs include working in the library, a research lab or the student union. Co-op or internship work that is part of your programme needs a co-op work permit — check the current rule on the IRCC site, as this changed in 2026.

Part-time work helps with living costs, but it will not cover international tuition. Plan your money as if you will earn little or nothing in the first term, while you settle in and find a job.

Staying after graduation

The post-graduation work permit (PGWP) is an open work permit: it lets you work for any employer in Canada, with no job offer needed. To be eligible you must have completed a programme of at least 8 months at a PGWP-eligible DLI, kept full-time status in every semester, and applied within 180 days of getting your final marks.

Your programmePGWP length
8 months to under 2 years (not a master's)Up to the length of your programme
2 years or moreUp to 3 years
Master's degree (at least 8 months), even if under 2 yearsUp to 3 years (rule in force since 15 February 2024)

The PGWP fee is CAD $255 (about ৳22,000): CAD $155 for the work permit plus CAD $100 for the open work permit holder fee. Since 1 November 2024, most PGWP applicants must also prove their English or French. University graduates (bachelor's, master's or doctoral) need CLB 7 in English or NCLC 7 in French in all four skills — roughly IELTS 6.0 in each skill. College graduates need CLB 5.

There is one more condition to check before you choose a programme. If you applied for your study permit on or after 1 November 2024 and you graduate from a college programme or a non-degree university programme, your field of study must be on IRCC's list of eligible fields, linked to occupations in long-term shortage. Bachelor's, master's and doctoral degree graduates have no field-of-study requirement. The PGWP cannot be extended except in narrow cases, such as when your passport expired before the full permit length — so plan your next step (skilled work, or an immigration programme) while the permit is still valid.

Documents checklist

  • Valid passport, with enough validity to cover your stay
  • Letter of acceptance from a designated learning institution (DLI)
  • Provincial attestation letter (PAL), unless you are exempt — for example, a master's or doctoral student at a public DLI from 1 January 2026
  • Proof of funds: GIC, bank statements for the past 6 months, education loan letter, tuition payment receipt, or scholarship letter
  • Academic transcripts and certificates (SSC, HSC, bachelor's if applicable)
  • English test result (IELTS / TOEFL / PTE) as the university requires
  • Statement of purpose and recommendation letters (for the university application)
  • Medical exam results, if IRCC asks for one
  • Police clearance certificate, if IRCC asks for one
  • Biometrics appointment, fee receipts and passport photos
  • Proof of ties to Bangladesh and your plan after study, for the visa officer
