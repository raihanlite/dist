Study in Germany from Bangladesh

19 Nov 2026 · 11 min read · Last checked 19 Nov 2026

Germany is the cheapest popular study destination for Bangladeshi students. Most public universities charge no tuition at all — you pay only a small semester contribution. The catch is the blocked account: you must show €11,904 (about ৳16.3 lakh) before your visa is issued. If your family can arrange that amount, a German degree can cost far less than one in the UK, USA or Australia.

Why study in Germany?

The main reason is cost. At most public universities in most German states, there is no tuition fee for a first bachelor's degree or a consecutive master's degree — German and foreign students pay the same: nothing, apart from the semester contribution. That contribution pays for student services and usually includes a public transport ticket for your city or region.

The second reason is quality and choice. Germany has many large, well-funded public universities, and more than 1,500 English-taught master's programmes are listed in the DAAD database. Engineering, computer science, natural sciences and business are strong, and a German degree is recognised across Europe.

The third reason is the path after graduation. Germany lets graduates stay for up to 18 months to look for qualified work, and you may take any job while you search. Few countries offer that much time.

The honest downside: the blocked account is a large sum to arrange before you travel, most bachelor's programmes are taught in German, and you need patience with German paperwork — appointments, registrations and residence permits all take time.

Tuition and living costs

Tuition is the part students usually misunderstand, so here it is plainly. Most public universities charge no tuition. You pay a semester contribution of roughly €150–350 each semester, and the exact amount is on each university's own website.

There are exceptions, and you must check them before you apply:

  • Baden-Württemberg charges non-EU students €1,500 per semester at its state universities — Heidelberg, Freiburg, Stuttgart, Tübingen and others. Doctoral students are exempt.
  • Bavaria lets its universities set fees for non-EU students. The Technical University of Munich, for example, charges non-EU students tuition for most programmes, so check the fee page of any Bavarian university you consider.
  • Private universities charge tuition, often several thousand euros a year, and are a different proposition entirely.

Living costs depend on the city. Munich and Hamburg are expensive; smaller cities in the east and north are cheaper. The blocked-account figure of €992 a month is a realistic planning number: rent takes the largest share, then health insurance, food and transport.

Note that the blocked account is not a fee — it is your own money, released to you at €992 a month after you arrive. The real cost of the first year is therefore lower than the total above: the €11,904 comes back to you as living money.

Admission requirements

German universities set their own admission rules, but the pattern is the same everywhere: your school and university results must match the German standard, and you must prove you can follow the language of instruction.

Bachelor's degree

German school-leaving takes 13 years; Bangladeshi HSC is 12 years. That means an HSC certificate alone is usually not enough for direct entry to a bachelor's programme. Most Bangladeshi applicants either complete a foundation year at a Studienkolleg (a one-year preparatory college, ending with an assessment test) or complete one year of university study in Bangladesh first. Check your exact position in the DAAD database and the anabin database before you apply — the rule depends on your subjects and grades.

Most bachelor's programmes are taught in German, so you will usually need German at B2 level or higher (TestDaF or Goethe certificate). English-taught bachelor's programmes exist but are fewer, and they are competitive.

Master's degree

You need a bachelor's degree in a related subject, usually with good grades. Most master's programmes for international students are taught in English, and universities typically ask for IELTS 6.0–6.5 or TOEFL equivalent. Some programmes also ask for GRE scores, a motivation letter, or work experience — read each programme page, not a general guide.

How to apply

Many universities take applications directly on their own websites. Others use uni-assist, a central service that checks your documents for several universities at once (it charges a fee per application). Apply to 4–6 universities. Deadlines are usually 15 July for the winter semester (starting October) and 15 January for the summer semester (starting April) — but each university sets its own date, so check every one.

Scholarships

Germany's main scholarship body is the DAAD (German Academic Exchange Service). DAAD scholarships are mostly for postgraduate study — master's, PhD and research stays — not for bachelor's degrees.

DAAD deadlines fall in the autumn for the following year — EPOS courses close on their own dates, mostly between August and December. That means you must start a year ahead: for a course starting in October 2027, most DAAD applications closed in late 2026. Search the DAAD scholarship database (funding-guide.de) and filter by Bangladesh, your level and your subject — do not apply to a programme you have not read in full.

One warning: DAAD does not pay tuition, because public universities charge none. The scholarship is a living-cost stipend, insurance and travel. If a university charges tuition (Baden-Württemberg, Bavaria, private), check whether the scholarship covers it before you accept.

Student visa

Bangladeshi citizens need a national student visa before travelling to Germany. You apply at the German Embassy in Dhaka, and you should start the process as soon as you have your admission letter — appointments and processing can take weeks, sometimes longer in busy months.

The blocked account (Sperrkonto)

The visa office needs proof that you can support yourself. The standard proof is a blocked account: a special German bank account into which you deposit €11,904 before your visa is issued. After you arrive, €992 a month is released to you. The amount is set by the German government from the maximum student support rate (BAföG). It is €992 a month for 2026, and the Federal Gazette notice of 31 August 2026 sets the same €992 a month for 2027. A draft reform of student support could raise the underlying rate during 2027, but it is not law yet — check the embassy's current figure before you transfer money.

Two providers currently accept new blocked-account applications: Fintiba and Expatrio. A third provider, Coracle, has paused new applications since August 2025. Open the account early — the transfer from Bangladesh can take time, and you need the confirmation for your visa appointment.

An alternative to the blocked account is a formal declaration of commitment (Verpflichtungserklärung) from a sponsor living in Germany, or a scholarship letter that covers your living costs. A regular bank statement from Bangladesh is not normally accepted on its own.

Visa fee and documents

The visa fee for a national (long-stay) visa is €75, paid at the embassy. Health insurance is mandatory from the first day — you need proof of cover for the visa, and after arrival you join German public or private student health insurance.

Working while studying

Non-EU students on a German student residence permit may work 140 full days or 280 half days per calendar year without extra permission. A day of up to 4 hours counts as a half day; more than 4 hours counts as a full day. This limit was raised from 120/240 days on 1 March 2024, so older guides that say 120 days are out of date.

Work as a student or research assistant at your own university does not count towards the day limit, and mandatory internships that are part of your course do not count either. During the lecture period, plan on roughly 20 hours a week at most — your studies come first, and the visa office can refuse to extend your permit if your grades suffer.

Germany's statutory minimum wage is €13.90 an hour in 2026 (about ৳1,900), rising to €14.60 from 1 January 2027. Typical student jobs are in cafés, shops, warehouses, delivery, tutoring and university research groups. Part-time work helps with living costs, but it cannot replace the blocked account — the visa office does not accept future earnings as financial proof.

Staying after graduation

After you finish your degree, you can apply for a residence permit to look for qualified work — the job-seeking permit under Section 20 of the Residence Act. It lasts up to 18 months, it is not renewable, and you must apply before your student residence permit expires. During those 18 months you may take up any type of job while you search for a qualified one.

Once you have a qualified job offer, you switch to a residence permit for skilled workers or an EU Blue Card. In 2026 the Blue Card needs a gross salary of €50,700 a year, or €45,934.20 for shortage occupations and for graduates whose degree is less than three years old. These thresholds are updated every year, so check the current figure before you negotiate a salary.

After two years of skilled work on a residence permit, with German at B1 level and pension contributions paid, you can usually apply for a permanent settlement permit. Learning German is therefore not just useful for daily life — it shortens the path to staying long term.

Documents checklist

  • Valid passport (at least 6 months beyond your planned stay)
  • Admission letter from the German university
  • Blocked account confirmation (€11,904) or scholarship letter or sponsor declaration
  • Health insurance proof
  • Visa application form and fee (€75)
  • Academic transcripts and certificates (SSC, HSC, bachelor's if applicable), translated if needed
  • Language certificate (IELTS/TOEFL for English-taught programmes, TestDaF/Goethe for German-taught)
  • Motivation letter and CV (as the university or embassy asks)
  • Passport photos (biometric, as specified by the embassy)
  • Proof of accommodation in Germany, if the embassy asks for it
  • APS certificate — not required for Bangladeshi applicants (it applies to China, India and Vietnam)
