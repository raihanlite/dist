Study in the USA from Bangladesh

19 Nov 2026 · 13 min read · Last checked 19 Nov 2026

The United States hosts more international students than any other country. Its universities offer a huge range of subjects, flexible degree structures and strong research facilities — but it is also one of the most expensive places to study, and the visa process needs careful planning. This guide gives Bangladeshi students the real numbers, the steps and the official sources, so you can plan without an agent.

Why study in the USA?

The USA has more than 4,000 colleges and universities, from small liberal arts colleges to large public research universities. That range is its biggest strength: whatever your subject and budget, there is usually a university that fits. Many Bangladeshi students choose the USA for computer science, engineering, business, data science and public health, where US degrees are widely recognised by employers at home and abroad.

American bachelor's degrees usually take four years, and the first two years include general subjects alongside your major. That flexibility helps if you are not yet sure of your exact field. Master's degrees usually take one to two years, and PhDs usually take four to six years. Most PhD students in science and engineering receive funding — a tuition waiver plus a monthly stipend for teaching or research work — but funding for bachelor's and master's students is competitive and never guaranteed.

There are real downsides to weigh. Tuition is high, health insurance is expensive, and the visa interview is strict: you must show clear funding and a genuine study plan. Rules have also been changing quickly in 2025–2026, including new fees and a proposed change to post-study work (see the callout below). Do not plan on rules staying the same between application and graduation.

Tuition and living costs

There is no single US tuition fee. Each university sets its own price, and international students usually pay the higher out-of-state rate at public universities. The College Board's published averages for 2025–26 give a useful starting point:

Highly selective private universities often charge $65,000 or more per year in tuition alone. On top of tuition, budget for housing and food, books, health insurance, transport and personal costs. Your university adds all of this up and prints the total on your Form I-20 — that total, not a national average, is the amount you must prove you can pay for the first year.

A realistic first-year budget at a typical public university is therefore roughly $45,000–$60,000 in total (tuition plus living costs), and $65,000–$85,000 or more at a private university. A community college start can cut the first two years sharply: you complete general courses there, then transfer to a four-year university for the final two years and graduate with that university's degree. Check that your credits will transfer before you choose this route.

Admission requirements

Each university decides its own admissions, so requirements differ. The usual list for Bangladeshi applicants is:

  • Academic results: SSC and HSC certificates and transcripts for a bachelor's degree; a bachelor's degree and transcripts for a master's. Strong grades in subjects related to your course matter most.
  • English test: IELTS or TOEFL. Most universities ask for IELTS 6.0–6.5 for a bachelor's and 6.5–7.0 for a master's, or the TOEFL equivalent. Competitive programmes ask for more. Check the exact score on each university's admissions page — there is no single national minimum.
  • Entrance tests: Many universities are now test-optional for the SAT and ACT at bachelor's level, meaning you may apply without them. Some graduate programmes still ask for the GRE or GMAT, especially in business and engineering. Check each programme.
  • Statement of purpose: A short essay explaining why you chose the subject, the university and what you plan to do after the degree. Write it yourself, in your own words.
  • Recommendation letters: Usually two or three, from teachers or, for master's applicants, employers who know your work well.
  • Financial documents: Bank statements or a sponsor's letter showing you can pay the first year's costs on your I-20. The university issues the I-20 only after it is satisfied on funding.

Apply to four to six universities: two ambitious, two realistic, two safe. Most universities open applications about a year before the course starts, for an August/September (fall) start. A smaller number offer a January (spring) start. Deadlines differ by university and are usually between December and February for the following fall — check each university's own page, and start at least 12 months ahead.

Scholarships and funding

Most funding for international students in the USA comes from the universities themselves, not from the government. There is no single national scholarship that covers everyone, and full funding for a bachelor's degree is rare. The main options are:

Fulbright in Bangladesh: the last round (for study starting September 2027) was announced by the US Embassy in Dhaka on 3 May 2026 and closed on 11 July 2026. It asked for IELTS 7.0 or TOEFL iBT 90, a four-year bachelor's degree from Bangladesh, and at least two years of relevant full-time work experience. The next round had not been announced when this guide was checked — watch the US Embassy Dhaka website and apply through the official Fulbright application system only. Fulbright scholars travel on a J-1 exchange visitor visa, not an F-1 visa, and the award has its own conditions, so read the embassy announcement in full.

Ask each university's admissions or financial aid office what funding it offers international students before you apply. Never pay anyone who promises a scholarship or a visa in exchange for a fee.

Student visa (F-1)

Most Bangladeshi students need an F-1 student visa. The steps, in order, are:

  1. Get your I-20. After you accept an offer and prove funding, your university's designated school official (DSO) issues Form I-20. Check that your name, course and funding figures on it are correct.
  2. Pay the SEVIS I-901 fee: $350 (about ৳43,400). Pay online at FMJfee.com using the SEVIS number on your I-20, and keep the receipt. You must pay this before the US Department of State issues your visa.
  3. Complete Form DS-160 online and pay the visa (MRV) fee: $185 (about ৳23,000). The MRV fee is non-refundable, even if the visa is refused.
  4. Book and attend the interview at the US Embassy in Dhaka. Student visas can be issued up to 365 days before your course starts. Interviews are short and focus on your study plan, your funding and your ties to Bangladesh. Answer honestly and in your own words.

Total before the interview: $535 (about ৳66,400). That is the SEVIS fee plus the MRV fee.

Budget for the new Visa Integrity Fee: a 2025 US law (Public Law 119-21, signed 4 July 2025) created a Visa Integrity Fee of at least $250 (about ৳31,000), charged when a visa is issued, not when you apply. It is being phased in through 2026 and is not collected uniformly at every embassy and consulate. Budget for it on top of the $535, and confirm whether your consulate is collecting it — and how to pay it — on the official State Department fee page or the US Embassy Dhaka website before your interview.

Financial proof: the USA does not set one flat amount for all students. You must show you can pay the full first-year cost printed on your I-20 — tuition plus living costs — with bank statements, a sponsor's letter, a scholarship letter or a loan, as your university and the embassy require. Keep the money in the account and keep the documents consistent: figures that do not match across your I-20, bank statement and DS-160 cause delays and refusals.

Documents for the interview: passport, I-20, DS-160 confirmation page, SEVIS fee receipt, MRV fee receipt, admission letter, academic transcripts and certificates, English test result, and financial documents. The embassy may ask for more — follow the instructions on the embassy website for Dhaka.

Working while studying

On an F-1 visa you may work on campus up to 20 hours a week while classes are in session, and full time during official breaks. You do not need a separate work permit for on-campus work, but you must confirm the job with your DSO first and you will need a Social Security Number. Off-campus work is not allowed without prior authorisation — working without it can end your student status.

Two other types of work are common:

  • Curricular Practical Training (CPT): an internship or work placement that is part of your course. Your DSO authorises it on your I-20. It usually needs one full academic year of study first, and 12 months or more of full-time CPT can remove your eligibility for post-completion OPT — ask your DSO before accepting a long placement.
  • Optional Practical Training (OPT): work in your field of study, usually after graduation (see below). You need your DSO's recommendation and an Employment Authorization Document from US Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) before you can start work.

Part-time work helps with living costs, but it will not cover tuition. Do not plan your budget around earnings from a campus job.

Staying after graduation: OPT

Optional Practical Training lets F-1 graduates work in the USA in a job related to their field of study for 12 months. If your degree is in a qualifying STEM field (science, technology, engineering or mathematics) on the Department of Homeland Security's STEM list, you can apply for a 24-month STEM OPT extension — up to 36 months in total. The STEM extension needs an employer enrolled in E-Verify and a training plan agreed with your DSO.

Apply for OPT through your university's international student office and USCIS, and do not start work until your Employment Authorization Document arrives. After OPT ends, staying longer needs a different visa — most commonly an employer-sponsored H-1B work visa, which is decided by a lottery and is never guaranteed. There is no automatic path from a US degree to permanent residence.

Warning — a proposed OPT fee, not a current fee: on 7 October 2026 the Department of Homeland Security proposed a rule under which schools would pay $70,000 before recommending a student for OPT for the first time, and $30,000 for each later recommendation, including a STEM extension. This is a proposal, not final law. The public comment period ran to 9 November 2026, and no student or school pays this fee today. Do not budget for it as a fact, and do not let anyone charge you for it. Check the Federal Register notice (Docket ICEB-2026-0100) or the Study in the States website for the current position before you rely on OPT plans.

Documents checklist

  • Valid passport (valid for at least 6 months beyond your planned stay)
  • SSC and HSC certificates and transcripts (plus bachelor's degree and transcripts for master's/PhD)
  • IELTS or TOEFL result, as your university requires
  • Statement of purpose and recommendation letters
  • Offer letter and Form I-20 from your university
  • SEVIS I-901 fee receipt ($350) and MRV fee receipt ($185)
  • DS-160 confirmation page and visa interview appointment
  • Financial proof matching the first-year total on your I-20 (bank statements, sponsor letter, scholarship letter)
  • Health insurance arranged as your university requires
  • Passport photos, as the embassy specifies
