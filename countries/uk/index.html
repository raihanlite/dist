Study in the UK from Bangladesh

19 Nov 2026 · 13 min read · Last checked 19 Nov 2026

The UK is one of the most popular destinations for Bangladeshi students. Its main attractions are short degrees — three years for a bachelor's and one year for most master's degrees — world-known universities, and a clear post-study work route. The cost is high, and the visa rules changed in 2026, so read the fees and the dates in this guide carefully before you plan.

Why study in the UK?

The UK's biggest advantage is time. A bachelor's degree in England, Wales and Northern Ireland usually takes three years, not four, and most taught master's degrees take one year. That saves a full year of tuition and living costs compared with a two-year master's elsewhere — often the difference that makes the UK affordable for a family budget.

The UK also has a simple, central system for undergraduate applications (UCAS), a large Bangladeshi community — especially people with roots in Sylhet — and universities whose degrees are well known to employers in Bangladesh and the Gulf. Teaching is in English, and many courses include a placement year or a dissertation project with an employer.

The downsides are cost and rule changes. London is one of the most expensive student cities in the world, visa fees and the health surcharge have risen sharply in recent years, and the post-study Graduate Route is being shortened for new applicants from 1 January 2027. Plan on the rules in force on the date you apply, not the rules your older brother or sister applied under.

Tuition and living costs

There is no single UK tuition fee. Each university sets its own price by course, and international students pay much more than home students. The British Council's Study UK guidance gives these broad ranges:

LevelTypical lengthTuition per yearAbout, in taka
Undergraduate degree3–4 years£11,400–£38,000About ৳18.6–62 lakh
Postgraduate (taught master's)1 year£9,000–£30,000About ৳14.7–49 lakh

Some courses sit well above these ranges — medicine and clinical degrees, and many MBAs, can cost £40,000 or more a year. Always use the fee printed on the university's own course page for your intake, not a range in a guide.

Living costs depend on the city. The British Council suggests roughly £900–£1,400 a month for living costs, with London at the top of that range. But the figure that matters for your visa is the Home Office maintenance figure (see the visa section): £1,529 a month in London and £1,171 elsewhere, for each month of your course up to nine months. That is the amount you must show in the bank — £13,761 for nine months in London, or £10,539 elsewhere, before the 30 November 2026 rise.

A worked example — a one-year master's outside London: tuition £20,000 + nine months' maintenance £10,539 + visa fee £558 + health surcharge £1,164 = about £32,261 (about ৳52.6 lakh) for the first year, before flights and the first month's setup costs. In London the same example is roughly £4,000–£5,000 more. Your own total will differ — use your university's fee and your city, not this example. Admission requirements

Undergraduate degrees — apply through UCAS. UCAS (the Universities and Colleges Admissions Service) is the central system for almost all full-time undergraduate courses. One application covers up to five course choices, with one personal statement and one reference. UCAS charges an application fee — £34.50 for up to five choices for 2027 entry — and its deadlines fall months before the course starts. For 2027 entry, the equal consideration deadline for most courses is 13 January 2027, and Oxford, Cambridge, medicine, dentistry and most veterinary courses closed earlier, on 15 October 2026. Confirm the deadline for your entry year on ucas.com; late applications are rarely considered.

Master's degrees — apply directly to the university. Postgraduate applications do not go through UCAS. You apply on each university's own website, and deadlines differ by university and course — popular courses close early, so apply months ahead. Most master's applicants also need an English test — compare IELTS and TOEFL to choose the right one.

The usual requirements for Bangladeshi applicants are:

  • Academic results: SSC and HSC for a bachelor's degree; a bachelor's degree for a master's. Universities publish the grades they expect for each course — check the course page, and ask the admissions office if your HSC grades are not listed.
  • English test: for a degree-level course you must show English at CEFR level B2. In practice that usually means IELTS for UKVI, with most universities asking for 6.0–6.5 overall for undergraduate and 6.5–7.0 for postgraduate study, sometimes with a minimum score in each skill. Your university may also accept other approved tests — check its English requirements page.
  • Personal statement: for UCAS, a structured statement answering UCAS's set questions about your subject choice and preparation (from September 2025 applications, this replaced the old free-form essay). For master's applications, a statement of purpose of similar length.
  • Reference: usually one, from a teacher or, for master's applicants, an employer.
  • Other tests: some courses need an admissions test or interview — for example medicine and dentistry, some law courses, and Oxford and Cambridge courses. Check the course page early, because test dates fall months before the application deadline.

Scholarships

Full scholarships for the UK exist, but they are very competitive and their deadlines fall about a year before the course starts. Check them before you shortlist universities, not after.

ScholarshipWho it is forWhat it covers
CheveningFuture leaders with at least 2,800 hours of work experience, for a one-year taught master'sFully funded: tuition, monthly living allowance, return flight and visa fee
Commonwealth ScholarshipsStudents from Commonwealth countries, including Bangladesh, for master's and PhD studyTuition, living costs and travel — check the Commonwealth Scholarship Commission site for the current awards
University scholarshipsStrong applicants to that universityPart of tuition, sometimes a fixed discount; each university sets its own awards and deadlines

Chevening: the UK government's global scholarship, funded by the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office. It funds a one-year master's at any UK university. The 2027/28 round opened on 4 August 2026 and closed on 6 October 2026 at 11:00 UTC, so it is already closed — the next round is expected to open in August 2027, but confirm the dates on chevening.org. You must apply for three different eligible master's courses and have at least 2,800 hours of work experience. Chevening does not fund undergraduate degrees.

Most Bangladeshi students who do not win a full scholarship pay with family funds, a bank loan or a university discount. Do not count on part-time work to pay tuition — the 20-hour limit and UK living costs make that impossible.

Student visa

You need a Student visa to study a degree in the UK. You apply online on GOV.UK, from outside the UK up to six months before your course starts. Decisions usually take about three weeks from outside the UK. The steps are:

  1. Get your CAS. After you accept an unconditional offer and pay any deposit, your university sends a Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS) — a reference number, not a paper letter. You must apply for your visa within six months of receiving it.
  2. Show the money. You must show your first-year course fees still unpaid (the figure is on your CAS) plus living costs: £1,529 a month in London or £1,171 a month elsewhere, for each month of your course up to nine months. The money must have been in the account for at least 28 days in a row, and the end of that 28-day period must fall within 31 days of your application date. If the balance drops below the required amount on any of those 28 days, the count restarts.
  3. Take the TB test. Bangladesh is on the UK's list of countries where applicants coming for more than six months need a tuberculosis test certificate from a Home Office-approved clinic. Use only a clinic on the GOV.UK list for Bangladesh — a certificate from any other clinic is not accepted — and note that it is valid for six months.
  4. Prove your English as your university and the visa rules require (see above).
  5. Pay and apply. Pay the visa fee and the health surcharge online, give your biometrics at a visa application centre, and submit your passport, CAS details, financial evidence and TB certificate.

The visa fee of £558 has applied since 8 April 2026. The health surcharge gives you access to the NHS while you study; the total depends on the length of your visa, which is usually your course length plus a few extra months, so a 12-month master's normally means paying for about a year and a half of surcharge — £1,164 at the student rate. The exact total is calculated when you apply, so check the figure on GOV.UK before you pay.

Maintenance is rising on 30 November 2026: under the Statement of Changes to the Immigration Rules (HC 584, 3 September 2026), the living-cost figures rise to £1,570 a month in London and £1,203 a month elsewhere for applications made on or after 30 November 2026. Applications made before that date are decided on the current figures (£1,529/£1,171). Which figure applies depends on your application date, not your course start date — so if you apply in December 2026 or later, show the new amounts: £14,130 for nine months in London, or £10,827 elsewhere. Always confirm the current figures on GOV.UK on the day you apply.

Financial evidence: Bangladesh is not on the list of nationalities that can skip submitting financial evidence, so expect to upload your bank documents with the application. The account can be yours or your parent's or legal guardian's — if it is a parent's account, you need proof of the relationship and their consent. A student loan or official sponsorship letter can also count, with evidence from the lender or sponsor.

Working while studying

On a full-time degree-level course at a university with a track record of compliance, you can work up to 20 hours a week in term time and full time during official vacations. Below degree level, the term-time limit is 10 hours a week. Part-time students cannot work at all. The limit covers all your jobs added together, not each job separately, and your university's calendar decides what counts as term time.

You cannot be self-employed, work as a professional sportsperson or coach, or claim public funds. Your visa states your exact work conditions — follow those, not this guide, if they differ. Part-time work at the UK minimum wage helps with food and transport, but it will not cover tuition or rent on its own.

Staying after graduation: the Graduate Route

The Graduate visa lets you stay in the UK and work after successfully completing an eligible course, with no job offer and no sponsor needed. You apply from inside the UK, after your university has told the Home Office you completed your course, and before your Student visa expires. You can work in most jobs and be self-employed on this visa, but you cannot extend it — to stay longer you must switch to another visa, such as a Skilled Worker visa with an employer sponsor.

When you applyHow long the Graduate visa lasts
On or before 31 December 20262 years (bachelor's or master's)
On or after 1 January 202718 months (bachelor's or master's)
Any time (PhD or other doctorate)3 years

The Graduate visa costs £937 (about ৳1,53,000), plus the health surcharge at the general rate of £1,035 a year (about ৳1,69,000 a year) — not the discounted student rate. A decision usually takes about eight weeks. Because the route shortens from 1 January 2027, a student starting a one-year master's in September 2026 and graduating in 2027 will be on the 18-month version. Plan your job search around that shorter window.

Documents checklist

  • Valid passport
  • Offer letter and CAS reference number from your university
  • SSC and HSC certificates and transcripts (plus bachelor's degree and transcripts for master's/PhD)
  • IELTS for UKVI or other approved English test result
  • Personal statement and reference (via UCAS for undergraduate)
  • Financial evidence: bank statements showing course fees plus maintenance, held for 28 days in a row
  • TB test certificate from a Home Office-approved clinic in Bangladesh
  • ATAS certificate, if your course and subject require it
  • Parental consent and birth certificate, if you are under 18
  • Visa fee (£558) and health surcharge payment receipts
  • Biometrics appointment at the visa application centre
