Study in Australia from Bangladesh

19 Nov 2026 · 13 min read · Last checked 19 Nov 2026

Australia is a popular choice for Bangladeshi students: good universities, a large Bangladeshi community in Sydney and Melbourne, and the right to work part time while you study. It is also one of the most expensive destinations, and the rules changed sharply in 2026 — the student visa fee rose to AUD $2,500, and from 2 October 2026 most new students can no longer bring family members with them. This guide explains the costs, the requirements and the steps in order, with figures checked at official Australian sources.

BDT figures in this guide use about ৳84 per AUD $1 (Wise mid-market rate, 18 November 2026). Exchange rates move daily, so treat every BDT figure as a rough guide. The Australian dollar figure is the one that matters.

Why study in Australia

Australia has 43 universities, and several rank among the best in the world. Popular subjects for Bangladeshi students include accounting, IT and computer science, engineering, nursing and public health, business and data science. Courses are practical, with work placements built into many degrees.

Three things draw Bangladeshi students. First, the large Bangladeshi community in Sydney, Melbourne and Brisbane makes settling in easier. Second, you can work up to 48 hours a fortnight during term time, which helps with living costs. Third, the Temporary Graduate visa lets you live and work full time in Australia for 2–3 years after you finish your degree.

The downsides are the cost and the distance. Tuition and living costs are high, flights from Dhaka take a full day, and the visa rules have tightened quickly — the visa fee has risen four times since 2024, and the 2026 changes affect family members and course changes. Budget carefully, and never plan your finances around part-time work.

Tuition and living costs

Australia does not publish one national tuition range, because every university sets its own fees. Study Australia, the government portal, says fees depend on your provider, your level of study and your city — and that you should check the exact fee on the course listing. As a rough guide, most university bachelor's and master's degrees cost about AUD $25,000–$45,000 a year (about ৳21–38 lakh). Medicine, dentistry and some MBA programmes cost far more. There may also be extra costs for course materials, such as lab coats or safety boots.

Admission requirements

Each university sets its own entry requirements, so check the course page of every university you apply to. In general, Bangladeshi applicants need:

  • For a bachelor's degree: HSC with good grades, usually in subjects related to the course. Some universities ask for specific HSC subjects, for example mathematics for engineering or IT. If your grades do not meet direct entry, many universities offer a foundation or diploma pathway that leads into the first or second year of the degree.
  • For a master's degree: a 4-year bachelor's degree in a related field, usually with a good CGPA. Some programmes ask for work experience, a portfolio or an interview.
  • For a PhD: a master's degree (or a bachelor's with first-class honours) in a related field, a research proposal, and usually contact with a supervisor before you apply.
  • English test: IELTS Academic is the most widely accepted test. The student visa needs a minimum of IELTS 6.0 overall, but universities usually ask for more: 6.0–6.5 for undergraduate and 6.5–7.0 for postgraduate study, with no band below 6.0. TOEFL and PTE are also accepted by most universities.
  • Other documents: academic transcripts and certificates, a statement of purpose, and recommendation letters for postgraduate study.

Most universities have two main intakes: February (Semester 1, the main intake) and July (Semester 2). Apply 8–12 months before the course starts. You apply directly on the university's website. Before you accept an offer, check that the course is registered on CRICOS (the Commonwealth Register of Institutions and Courses for Overseas Students) — only CRICOS courses can enrol international students on a student visa. After you accept the offer and pay the deposit, the university issues a Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE), which you need for the visa application.

Scholarships

Full scholarships for undergraduate study in Australia are rare, and competition is strong. Most Bangladeshi students pay full tuition. Check these options before you apply — deadlines fall months before the course starts.

Be careful: no genuine scholarship asks you to pay a fee to apply, and no agent can guarantee you a scholarship or a visa. If someone promises a "100% scholarship" or a "guaranteed visa" for a payment, walk away.

The biggest fully funded option is the Australia Awards Scholarships.

Student visa (subclass 500)

Bangladeshi citizens need a Student visa (subclass 500) to study in Australia. You apply online through ImmiAccount after you have a Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) from your university. Apply as early as you can — processing can take several weeks, and you also need time for a medical exam and biometrics.

Fees

The visa application charge is from AUD $2,500 (about ৳2.1 lakh). It was AUD $2,000 before July 2026, AUD $1,600 before that, and AUD $710 before July 2024 — the fee has risen sharply, so confirm the current charge on the Department of Home Affairs Visa Pricing Estimator before you pay. A lower charge applies in limited cases, such as eligible Pacific Island, Timor-Leste and ASEAN citizens.

Financial proof

You must show you have enough money for your tuition, living costs, travel and any school costs for accompanying children. The living-cost amount for a single applicant is AUD $29,710 a year (about ৳25 lakh), in force since 10 May 2024. You can show this with bank statements, an education loan from a bank, a scholarship letter, or proof of income. You must also show you can pay the first year of tuition. Do not plan to rely on part-time work — Home Affairs says students should not rely on work to support themselves while in Australia.

Genuine Student requirement

Your application is assessed against the Genuine Student requirement: you must show that you genuinely intend to study in Australia, that you understand the course and why you chose it, and that you have realistic plans for after your studies. You may be asked to write a statement and attend an interview. Your English test, your academic history and your financial documents all form part of this assessment.

Health cover and health checks

You must hold Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) for the whole length of your stay, unless you fall into an exemption category. Buy it before you apply for the visa, and keep it active while you are in Australia. You may also need a medical exam and a police clearance certificate.

Big change from 2 October 2026: the government announced on 17 September 2026 that most new student visa applicants can no longer include a partner or children as secondary applicants. Exceptions apply for certain courses (for example PhDs), for Pacific and ASEAN nationals, and for some other listed cases — and families already in Australia on a visa are not separated. The same limits apply to the Temporary Graduate visa. Because the detailed rules are still being rolled out, check the current subclass 500 page on the Home Affairs website before you make any family plans. The 2 October 2026 changes also tightened course changes: students onshore can generally only move to a higher qualification, not to a lower one.

Working while studying

Student visa holders can work up to 48 hours a fortnight while their course is in session. A fortnight is 14 days starting on a Monday — so you could work, for example, 20 hours in one week and 28 in the next, as long as the 14-day total is 48 or less. During course breaks you can work unlimited hours. Students in a master's by research or a doctoral degree can work unlimited hours once their course has started. You cannot work before your course starts (unless you already held another visa that allowed work).

You need a Tax File Number (TFN) before you start work, and your employer must pay you at least the legal minimum wage. You can hold more than one job, as long as the total hours stay within the limit. Working more than the limit is a breach of your visa conditions and can lead to your visa being cancelled. Part-time work helps with living costs, but it will not cover international tuition — plan your money as if you will earn little or nothing in the first term.

Staying after graduation

The Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485) lets you live, study and work full time in Australia after you finish. There are two main streams for graduates:

Your qualificationStay on a subclass 485
Bachelor's degreeUp to 2 years
Master's by courseworkUp to 2 years
Master's by research or PhDUp to 3 years
Diploma, trade or associate degree (vocational stream)Up to 18 months

To be eligible for the Post-Higher Education Work stream you must have completed a CRICOS-registered degree that meets the Australian study requirement — at least 92 weeks of registered study completed over at least 16 months — and you must apply within 6 months of completing your course. Your English must meet the graduate visa standard: IELTS 6.5 overall, with at least 5.5 in each skill (or an equivalent test score). Most applicants must also be under 35 when they apply. Check the subclass 485 page on the Home Affairs website for the current fee and the full rules before you apply — charges for this visa rose sharply in 2026.

The 485 visa does not lead directly to permanent residence. It gives you time to gain skilled Australian work experience, which can help if you later apply for a skilled visa. Plan your next step early: the 485 cannot be extended (except through the regional second visa), so use the time well.

Documents checklist

  • Valid passport, with enough validity to cover your stay
  • Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) from a CRICOS-registered course
  • Proof of funds: bank statements, education loan letter, scholarship letter, or proof of income
  • English test result (IELTS / TOEFL / PTE) — minimum IELTS 6.0 overall for the visa
  • Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) arranged for the full stay
  • Genuine Student statement, if asked for
  • Academic transcripts and certificates (SSC, HSC, bachelor's if applicable)
  • Statement of purpose and recommendation letters (for the university application)
  • Medical exam results, if Home Affairs asks for one
  • Police clearance certificate, if asked for
  • Biometrics appointment and visa fee receipt
  • Proof of ties to Bangladesh and your plans after study, for the visa assessment
