Chinese Government Scholarship (CSC) — Full Guide for Bangladeshi Students

19 Nov 2026 · 7 min read · Last checked 19 Nov 2026

The Chinese Government Scholarship (CSC), run by the China Scholarship Council under China's Ministry of Education, is one of the largest fully funded scholarship programmes in the world — around 30,000 awards a year to students from more than 180 countries. For Bangladeshi students it funds bachelor's, master's and PhD study at Chinese universities, with tuition, accommodation, insurance and a monthly stipend. There is no bond and no repayment. This guide explains the two application routes, what it pays, and the new test that bachelor's applicants now need to take.

The two routes: Type A and Type B

CSC has two application channels, and you can apply through both at the same time. They are not the same process, so do not confuse them.

DetailType A — Embassy channelType B — University channel
Apply throughThe Chinese Embassy in Dhaka (check the embassy's current notice for how it collects applications)Directly to a Chinese university that holds CSC places, through campuschina.org
How it worksThe embassy recommends candidates to CSC for final approvalThe university nominates you to CSC for final approval
UniversitiesYou list up to 2 universities in your applicationYou apply to up to 3 universities

Both routes use the same online system at campuschina.org. Every legitimate CSC application goes through that system, and each embassy or university has its own agency number that you enter when you register. Do not use any other website to apply — copycat sites and agents are common.

New for bachelor's applicants: the CSCA test. From the 2026/27 cycle, bachelor's degree applicants must take the China Scholastic Competency Assessment (CSCA) and submit a score report before starting their scholarship application. This is a standardised test run through csca.cn. Master's and PhD applicants do not need it. Check the current year's embassy or university notice to confirm whether it applies to you.

Who can apply from Bangladesh

  • Citizenship: you must be a Bangladeshi citizen in good health.
  • Education: bachelor's applicants need HSC; master's applicants need a bachelor's degree; PhD applicants need a master's degree.
  • Age: under 25 for bachelor's, under 35 for master's, under 40 for PhD.
  • Grades: competitive programmes usually expect a GPA of 3.0/4.0 or above for master's study, and higher for the most competitive universities. There is no single published minimum — each university sets its own bar.
  • Language: many programmes are taught in Chinese, and some in English. Chinese-taught programmes usually require HSK (Chinese proficiency) at a level the university specifies; English-taught programmes usually ask for IELTS or TOEFL. Check the programme page.
  • Not funded: CSC generally does not fund MBA or MBBS degrees under this scheme.

What CSC covers — and what it does not

The award covers tuition in full, free dormitory accommodation (or a housing allowance if you live off campus), medical insurance, and the monthly stipend above. A settling-in allowance is also paid on arrival at some universities.

The main gap is airfare: under the embassy route, CSC does not pay your flights to and from China. Budget for return flights yourself. The stipend is modest but liveable in most Chinese cities outside Beijing and Shanghai, especially since your biggest costs — tuition and housing — are already covered.

How to apply, step by step

  1. Choose your route. If you have a university in mind, contact it first — a pre-admission letter strengthens a Type B application. If not, use the Type A embassy route.
  2. Register on campuschina.org. Create an account on the Chinese Government Scholarship Information System. Select the programme category (Type A or Type B) and enter the agency number for the embassy or university.
  3. Prepare your documents. Notarised transcripts and certificates, a study plan or research proposal (about 800 words for master's, 1,500 for PhD is a common guide), two recommendation letters from professors or associate professors, a passport copy, and a health examination form.
  4. Bachelor's applicants: take the CSCA. Register at csca.cn and sit the test before you submit your scholarship application.
  5. Submit before the deadline. Embassy deadlines mostly fall in February; university deadlines run from January to April. Each deadline is set by the embassy or university — there is no single national date.
  6. Wait for the result. CSC makes the final decision after the embassy or university nominates you. Results usually come in June–July, for a September start.

Documents checklist

  • CSC online application form (from campuschina.org, with the correct agency number)
  • Notarised academic transcripts and degree certificates
  • Study plan or research proposal
  • Two recommendation letters from professors or associate professors
  • Passport copy (valid for at least 6 months beyond your planned stay)
  • Foreigner Physical Examination Form (health check)
  • Pre-admission letter from a Chinese university, if you have one (Type B)
  • CSCA score report (bachelor's applicants, from 2026/27)
  • Language certificate — HSK for Chinese-taught programmes, IELTS/TOEFL for English-taught ones

Common mistakes

  • Applying on the wrong website. campuschina.org is the only official portal. Agent sites that charge fees for CSC applications are not legitimate.
  • Wrong agency number. Each embassy and university has its own agency number. Entering the wrong one sends your application to the wrong place.
  • Skipping the CSCA (bachelor's). From 2026/27, bachelor's applicants need a CSCA score before they apply. Without it, your application is incomplete.
  • A generic study plan. A study plan that could apply to any university in any country is a weak application. Name the programme, the professors, and what you want to research.
  • Forgetting the airfare. CSC does not pay your flights. Budget for them — discovering this after you win the award is a bad surprise.
  • Not notarising documents. CSC requires notarised copies of transcripts and certificates. Plain photocopies are rejected.

Chinese-taught or English-taught?

One decision shapes your whole CSC experience: the language of instruction. English-taught programmes let you start your degree immediately, and they are common at master's and PhD level, especially in engineering, business and science. Chinese-taught programmes usually require HSK level 4 or above, and if your Chinese is not yet strong enough, many universities offer a year of language preparation before the degree starts. Learning Chinese is a serious time commitment, but it opens far more programmes — including the most competitive ones — and daily life in China is much easier with the language. If you choose an English-taught programme, still learn basic Mandarin before you go: it makes housing, banking, hospitals and everyday life manageable, and it is the skill employers value most when you return. Whichever route you take, confirm the language requirement on the programme's own page before you apply, because the same university can require different levels for different programmes.
