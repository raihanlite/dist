Commonwealth Scholarships and Erasmus Mundus — Two Routes Compared

19 Nov 2026 · 8 min read · Last checked 19 Nov 2026

Two of the best-known fully funded routes for Bangladeshi master's students look similar from a distance — both pay for a master's degree in Europe, both are very competitive, both have autumn deadlines. But they work differently: Commonwealth Scholarships are UK awards run by the Commonwealth Scholarship Commission, while Erasmus Mundus is an EU programme that sends you to two or more European countries for a joint degree. This guide covers both, so you can decide which one fits you — or apply to both.

Part 1: Commonwealth Scholarships

The Commonwealth Scholarship Commission (CSC) runs three master's schemes. For Bangladeshi students, two matter most: the main Commonwealth Master's Scholarships and the Commonwealth Shared Scholarships.

Commonwealth Master's Scholarships (main round)

The main round funds a one-year full-time master's at a UK university. You do not apply to the CSC directly — you apply through your country's nominating agency, which runs its own earlier call and forwards its chosen candidates to the CSC. For the 2027/28 round, CSC applications closed at 16:00 BST on 20 October 2026, with nominations due by December 2026 and results by July 2027. The nominating agency's own deadline is always earlier than the CSC deadline, so start with the agency, not the CSC.

Eligibility: you must be a citizen of an eligible Commonwealth country (Bangladesh is one), and by September of the year you start, hold at least an upper second-class (2:1) bachelor's degree — or a lower second-class (2:2) degree plus a relevant postgraduate qualification, usually a master's. You must also show you could not afford to study in the UK without the scholarship.

Commonwealth Shared Scholarships

Shared Scholarships are funded jointly by the CSC and participating UK universities, for students from least developed and lower- and middle-income Commonwealth countries who could not otherwise afford a UK master's. They fund full-time taught master's degrees only — no PhD, no distance learning.

For the 2027 round, applications opened on Tuesday 10 November 2026 and close at 16:00 GMT on Tuesday 8 December 2026, for courses starting in September or October 2027. You apply through the participating UK university, not through a nominating agency, and you must also apply to the university for admission to the same course separately. The course must be on the CSC's approved list for that year, and the university sends its chosen names to the CSC in March 2027.

The package: full tuition, return airfare, and a monthly stipend of £1,712 — or £2,000 if your university is in the London metropolitan area — plus a thesis grant, a study travel grant and other small allowances.

How to apply for Commonwealth (step by step)

  1. Decide which scheme fits. Main round if you want any eligible UK course and can go through the nominating agency; Shared if your course is on the approved list and you apply through the university.
  2. Main round: watch for the national nominating agency's call in Bangladesh — it opens before the CSC deadline and closes earlier. Apply through the agency.
  3. Shared round: pick an approved course, apply to the university for admission, and submit the CSC application through the university's route before the December deadline.
  4. Wait for the result. Main-round results come by July; Shared-round nominations go to the CSC in March.

Part 2: Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters

An Erasmus Mundus Joint Master (EMJM) is a single master's degree run by a consortium of universities in two or more European countries. You study in at least two countries — for example, a semester in France, a semester in Spain — and graduate with a joint or multiple degree. There are more than 150 EMJM programmes, covering fields from public policy and law to engineering, data science and environmental studies.

What the scholarship pays

The EMJM scholarship is a single monthly contribution of up to €1,400, for a maximum of €33,600 over a 24-month programme, plus a full tuition waiver and insurance. The €1,400 is one payment, not two — it is meant to cover travel, visa, installation and living costs together. Some guides list the stipend and the allowance separately, which makes it look like €2,800 a month. It is not. Budget your flight and visa costs from that same €1,400 until the consortium confirms otherwise in writing.

Who can apply

  • Degree: a bachelor's degree, completed by the programme's deadline (some programmes require it by the application date, not by the start date — check).
  • No age limit and no nationality restriction — EMJM is open to students from all countries, including Bangladesh.
  • English: IELTS 6.5–7.0 or equivalent is typical, but each programme sets its own requirement.
  • One rule to know: if you have already received an EMJM scholarship, you cannot receive another one.

How to apply

There is no separate scholarship application. You apply to the programme through the consortium's own portal, and in the application form you say whether you want to be considered for funding. Applicants who want the scholarship must meet the programme's earliest deadline — later deadlines are for self-funded students only, and they do not reopen scholarship ranking.

Deadlines are set by each programme and usually fall between October and January. For the 2027 intake, for example, the Mundus MAPP public policy programme's scholarship deadline was 1 December 2026, NOHA humanitarian action was 15 December 2026, and the European Master in Law and Economics was 31 December 2026 for non-European applicants. Your programme's date is the only one that matters — check it on the programme's own website.

Documents checklist (both programmes)

  • Academic transcripts and degree certificate
  • CV (Europass format is standard for Erasmus Mundus)
  • Letter of motivation — tailored to the specific programme, not generic
  • Two recommendation letters
  • English test result (IELTS / TOEFL, as the programme requires)
  • Passport copy
  • For Commonwealth: proof that you could not afford UK study without the award; for the main round, the nominating agency's own form
  • For Erasmus Mundus: proof of residence, if the programme asks for it

Common mistakes

  • Commonwealth: applying to the CSC directly. The main round only accepts candidates sent by nominating agencies. A direct application goes nowhere.
  • Commonwealth Shared: forgetting the university application. The scholarship application does not admit you to the university. You must apply for admission separately, to the same course.
  • Erasmus Mundus: using the late deadline. Scholarship applicants must meet the earliest deadline. The spring deadlines are for self-funded students only.
  • Erasmus Mundus: double-counting the money. €1,400 a month is the whole contribution — travel, visa, installation and living. Do not budget as if flights are paid separately.
  • One motivation letter for every programme. Each EMJM consortium and each Commonwealth course wants to know why their programme. A copied-and-pasted letter is easy to spot.
