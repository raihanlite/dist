Chevening Scholarship UK for Bangladeshi Students

19 Nov 2026 · 7 min read · Last checked 19 Nov 2026

Chevening is the UK government's flagship scholarship for future leaders. It funds a one-year master's degree at any UK university — tuition, living costs, flights and visa — and Bangladesh is one of its active countries. It is also one of the most competitive scholarships in the world: tens of thousands apply each year for a few thousand places. This guide explains who can apply, what it covers, and how the timeline works.

Who can apply from Bangladesh

  • Citizenship: you must be a citizen of Bangladesh (Chevening is open to citizens of Chevening-eligible countries, and Bangladesh is one). You must return to Bangladesh for at least two years after your award ends.
  • Degree: an undergraduate degree that qualifies you for a UK master's programme.
  • Work experience: at least 2,800 hours. Chevening counts full-time and part-time employment, paid and voluntary work, and internships. Keep records — you will need to list each role with dates and hours.
  • Courses: you apply to three different eligible master's courses at UK universities. They can be at the same university or three different ones, but they must be full-time, taught master's programmes starting in the autumn.
  • English: there is no separate Chevening English test. Your chosen universities set their own English requirements, and you must meet them to get an unconditional offer.
  • Who should not apply: if you have already studied in the UK on a UK government-funded scholarship, or if you hold British or dual British citizenship, you are not eligible. Check the full eligibility rules on chevening.org before you start.

What Chevening covers

Chevening describes itself as fully funded, and the package is generous: full tuition fees for your master's, a monthly living allowance (the amount depends on whether you study in London or elsewhere), a return economy flight to the UK, the cost of your student visa, and arrival and departure allowances. There is no separate application fee — applying to Chevening is free.

What it does not cover: your university application costs (if any), English test fees, or costs for family members. If dependants travel with you, you fund that yourself.

The timeline — and why it is a year long

Chevening runs on a fixed annual cycle. The 2027/28 round opened on 4 August 2026 and closed on 6 October 2026 at 11:00 UTC. The next round, for study starting in September 2028, is expected to open in August 2027 — confirm the exact dates on chevening.org when the cycle approaches.

The lesson: you apply to Chevening a full year before you start studying, and you apply to your three UK universities in parallel. You do not need an offer to apply for Chevening, but you must hold an unconditional offer from one of your three courses by the July deadline.

How to apply, step by step

  1. Check eligibility (June–July). Use the eligibility checker on chevening.org and confirm Bangladesh is eligible for the coming round.
  2. Choose three courses. Pick three eligible master's courses that fit your career goals. Check each university's entry requirements and deadlines — some close early.
  3. Apply to the universities. Submit your university applications separately, through each university's own system. Chevening does not apply for you.
  4. Write the four essays. The Chevening application is built around four essays: leadership and influence, networking, study in the UK, and career plan. Successful applicants usually spend weeks on these, not days.
  5. Submit before the deadline. The portal closes at the published time and does not accept late applications. Submit at least a few days early — the system slows down in the final hours.
  6. Interview. If shortlisted, you are interviewed in Dhaka. The interview tests the same four themes as the essays — leadership, networking, why the UK, and your career plan.
  7. Secure your offer. If selected, you must hold an unconditional offer from one of your three courses by the July deadline.

Documents checklist

  • Valid passport or national ID
  • Undergraduate degree certificate and transcripts
  • Two references (submitted only if you are shortlisted for interview)
  • Three chosen UK master's courses, with the universities' entry requirements checked
  • Work history with dates and hours for each role (to reach 2,800 hours)
  • English test result, if your chosen universities require one
  • Unconditional offer from one of your three courses (by the July deadline)

Common mistakes

  • Writing about yourself instead of your impact. Chevening selects future leaders, not the best students. Essays that list grades and courses score poorly; essays that show what you changed, who you influenced, and what you will do next score well.
  • One generic career plan. "I will return to Bangladesh and work in development" is not a plan. Name the organisation, the role, and the problem you will work on.
  • Choosing three unrelated courses. Your three courses should tell one coherent story about your career. Three random choices suggest you have no plan.
  • Waiting for the offer before applying. You apply to Chevening and to universities at the same time. If you wait for an offer first, you miss the Chevening deadline.
  • Missing the 2,800 hours. Count your hours before you apply. Part-time work and volunteering count, but you must be able to evidence them.

Life as a Chevening Scholar

The award is not only money. Chevening Scholars join a global network of more than 55,000 alumni in over 160 countries, including senior figures in government, business, media and civil society across South Asia. During your year in the UK, the Chevening Secretariat runs events, visits and networking sessions for scholars, and the connections you make with other scholars — from Bangladesh and from everywhere else — often outlast the degree itself. After you return home, you join the local Chevening alumni network, which runs its own events and mentoring. When Chevening interviews you, it is selecting not just a student for one year but a future member of that network, so your career plan and your willingness to stay involved matter as much as your grades.

Plan for the next round now: the 2027/28 round closed on 6 October 2026. If you missed it, the 2028/29 round is expected to open in August 2027. Use the months in between to build work experience, shortlist courses, and draft your essays — the strongest applications are written over months, not in the final week.
