Australia Awards Scholarships for Bangladeshi Students

19 Nov 2026 · 7 min read · Last checked 19 Nov 2026

Australia Awards Scholarships are the Australian Government's long-term awards for students from partner countries, including Bangladesh. They fund full-time undergraduate or postgraduate study at Australian universities and TAFE institutions — tuition, flights, living costs and health cover. They are among the most generous scholarships open to Bangladeshis, and also among the most competitive, because one award can be worth several years of full funding.

Who can apply from Bangladesh

Bangladesh is on the Australia Awards list for South Asia, and Bangladeshi citizens can apply for undergraduate and postgraduate awards. Applicants are usually expected to be working in a field related to their proposed study, and the award is meant to support people who will contribute to Bangladesh's development when they return. Compare IELTS and TOEFL to choose the right test for you.

  • Citizenship: you must be a citizen of Bangladesh and resident there when you apply.
  • Level: undergraduate applicants need HSC; postgraduate applicants need a bachelor's degree. PhD applicants need a master's or strong research background.
  • English: postgraduate study usually requires IELTS 6.5 overall with no band below 6.0, or an equivalent TOEFL or PTE score. Undergraduate requirements are set by the institution.
  • Development focus: your proposed study should relate to a priority area for Bangladesh — the country page lists the priority fields for each round. Applications that do not match the priorities are unlikely to succeed.
  • No fees: there is no application fee at any stage. Anyone who asks you to pay for an Australia Awards application is running a scam — DFAT says so explicitly.

What the award covers

DFAT lists the following benefits as generally applying to Australia Awards Scholarships:

  • Full tuition fees for the duration of your course.
  • One return economy-class airfare to and from Australia.
  • Establishment allowance — a one-time payment on arrival for accommodation, textbooks and study materials.
  • Contribution to Living Expenses (CLE) — a fortnightly payment towards basic living costs, at a rate set by DFAT. The exact rate is not published on the DFAT website and is confirmed in your individual award letter.
  • Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) for the duration of the award.
  • Introductory Academic Program and other support services, including tutoring in some cases.

What it does not cover: your family's costs, or any costs if you stay in Australia after the award ends. The two-year return obligation is a condition of the award, not a suggestion.

The timeline

For study starting in 2027, applications for most participating countries — including Bangladesh — opened on 1 February 2026 and closed on 30 April 2026 at 14:00 AEST. That round is now closed. DFAT has not yet published dates for the next round; when it does, they appear on its opening and closing dates page. Based on the usual cycle, the next round is expected to open in February 2027 for study starting in 2028 — but treat that as an expectation, not a confirmed date.

How to apply, step by step

  1. Read the Bangladesh country page. Each participating country has its own page on australiaawards.gov.au with priority fields, eligibility details and the application link. Start there, not on a general scholarship list.
  2. Check the priority fields. Your proposed course must fit Bangladesh's priority areas for that round. A strong application in the wrong field will not be selected.
  3. Apply online. Applications are submitted through the Australia Awards online system (OASIS). You apply for the scholarship and for university admission through the same process — you do not apply to the university separately first.
  4. Interviews. Shortlisted candidates are interviewed in Bangladesh, usually by a panel including Australian and Bangladeshi representatives.
  5. Offer and pre-departure. Successful candidates receive an offer, complete medical checks and pre-departure briefings, and travel to Australia for the start of the academic year.

Documents checklist

  • Academic transcripts and certificates (SSC, HSC, bachelor's / master's as applicable)
  • English test result (IELTS / TOEFL / PTE, as the country page requires)
  • Passport copy
  • CV showing work experience relevant to your proposed study
  • Referee reports (usually two)
  • Development impact statement — how your study will benefit Bangladesh when you return
  • Any additional documents listed on the Bangladesh country page for that round

Common mistakes

  • Applying outside the priority fields. Australia Awards is a development programme, not a general scholarship. Your course must match the priorities published for Bangladesh.
  • Weak development case. The selection panel asks one question above all: what will this person do for Bangladesh after they return? An application with no answer to that question fails.
  • Missing the April deadline. The window is three months, but the online system is busy at the end. Submit well before 30 April.
  • Paying someone to apply. There is no fee at any stage. Agents who charge for Australia Awards applications are not authorised by DFAT.
  • Ignoring the two-year rule. If you plan to stay in Australia after your degree, this is the wrong scholarship. The return obligation is enforced, and breaking it creates a debt for the full cost of the award.

Write your development impact statement the way you would write a statement of purpose — specific about what you will do for Bangladesh after you return.

After the award: returning home and the alumni network

When your course ends, the award requires you to leave Australia for at least two years. This is not a punishment — it is the point of the programme. Australia Awards exist so that graduates take their skills home and use them in Bangladesh's public service, universities, businesses and NGOs. Returning scholars join the Australia Awards alumni network in Bangladesh, which runs professional development events, mentoring and networking, and many alumni go on to senior roles in government and development organisations. Before you apply, think honestly about whether you are ready for that commitment: if your plan is to settle in Australia permanently after your degree, this scholarship will work against you, because the return obligation is enforced and breaking it creates a debt for the full cost of the award. Apply because you want to study in Australia and bring the degree home — not as a migration route. If you win the award, you will still need a student visa to enter Australia.

If Australia Awards is closed, look at university awards too: Australian universities run their own scholarships for international students, and research students can apply for the Research Training Program (RTP), which pays a stipend plus a tuition offset through the university's own research rounds. These are separate from Australia Awards and have their own deadlines.
