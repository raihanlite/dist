DAAD Scholarships in Germany for Bangladeshi Students

19 Nov 2026 · 7 min read · Last checked 19 Nov 2026

DAAD — the German Academic Exchange Service — is the largest funding organisation for international students in Germany. For Bangladeshi students, the most relevant programme is EPOS (Development-Related Postgraduate Courses), which funds master's and, in exceptional cases, doctoral study for professionals from developing countries. Germany's public universities mostly charge no tuition, so a DAAD award is mainly a living stipend — and €992 a month covers life in most German cities.

Who can apply from Bangladesh

EPOS is not a scholarship you apply for directly. It is a status DAAD gives to a list of selected postgraduate courses. You apply to a course on that list, and the course nominates its chosen candidates to DAAD. Bangladesh is on DAAD's list of eligible developing countries, so Bangladeshi graduates can apply.

  • Degree: a bachelor's degree, usually completed within the last 6 years.
  • Work experience: at least 2 years of relevant professional experience after your degree. This is a hard requirement for EPOS — fresh graduates cannot apply.
  • Field: your course must be on the EPOS list of development-related postgraduate courses. The list covers economics, agriculture, environment, engineering, public health, social sciences, education and related fields, at more than 40 German universities.
  • English: most EPOS courses are taught in English and ask for IELTS 6.0–6.5 or equivalent. Some courses ask for German instead — check the course page.
  • Typical candidate: a mid-career professional working in government, an NGO, a company or a university in Bangladesh, whose work relates to development.

What DAAD covers

EPOS pays a monthly amount of €992 for master's students and €1,300 for doctoral candidates (€1,400 from February 2026), for the standard length of your course. On top of that, DAAD pays for health, accident and personal liability insurance, and a travel allowance unless another source covers your flight. In some cases you can also receive a monthly rent subsidy and an allowance for accompanying family members.

Because the payment matches Germany's official living-cost requirement (€992 a month, or €11,904 a year for the blocked account), the DAAD award letter also serves as your proof of finance for the student visa — you do not need to open a blocked account. You still pay the €75 visa fee yourself.

What EPOS does not pay: the semester contribution (€150–€350 per semester, which usually includes a public transport ticket). Budget for that separately.

If you are a fresh graduate without work experience, the general master's study scholarship — not EPOS — is the right DAAD route. Use the country-of-origin filter in the DAAD scholarship database to see which programmes are open to applicants from Bangladesh.

How to apply, step by step

  1. Find your course. Open the EPOS course list on daad.de and pick 1–3 courses that match your degree and work experience. Check each course's own deadline — they differ, and most fall between August and October.
  2. Apply to the course, not to DAAD. You submit your application — including the DAAD application form — directly to the university running the course. There is no separate DAAD application at this stage.
  3. Course selection. The university selects its candidates and forwards them to DAAD. If you are not selected by the course, DAAD never sees your application.
  4. DAAD decision. DAAD makes the final funding decision, usually a few months after the course deadline.
  5. Visa. With the DAAD award letter, apply for a German student visa at the German Embassy in Dhaka. The award letter replaces the blocked account as proof of finance.

Start a year early: EPOS courses for an October start usually close between August and October the year before. That means you should be shortlisting courses and preparing documents 12–14 months before you want to start studying. A course that closes in August will not wait for your IELTS result in September.

Documents checklist

  • DAAD application form (downloaded from the DAAD database entry for your programme)
  • CV in Europass format, signed and dated
  • Letter of motivation, signed and dated, explaining your development-related work
  • Letter of recommendation from your employer, on letterhead
  • Academic transcripts and degree certificates, attested
  • English test result (IELTS 6.0–6.5 or equivalent, as the course requires)
  • Employment certificates proving at least 2 years of relevant work experience
  • Passport copy

Common mistakes

  • Applying to DAAD instead of the course. For EPOS, DAAD does not accept direct applications. Applications go to the university running the course.
  • Applying as a fresh graduate. EPOS requires 2 years of professional experience. Without it, your application is rejected outright — use the general master's scholarship instead.
  • Choosing a course that is not on the EPOS list. Only the listed courses are funded. A regular master's at the same university is not an EPOS course.
  • One deadline for all courses. Every EPOS course sets its own deadline. Check each course page — do not assume a single national date.
  • Weak motivation letter. EPOS funds people who will use the degree for development work back home. A letter that only talks about your own career, with no link to Bangladesh's needs, is a weak application.

After EPOS: staying in Germany

Your DAAD funding ends when your course ends, but your time in Germany does not have to. Graduates of German universities can apply for an 18-month residence permit to look for a job related to their degree, and during that period you may work without restriction. Many EPOS graduates use this time to find work with a German company, an international organisation, or a development agency — and the work experience you built before your master's, plus a German degree, is a strong combination. Others return to Bangladesh and use the degree in their old organisation or a new one, which is what EPOS is designed for: the programme's purpose is to train specialists who contribute to development in their home countries. Either path is valid, but decide early — job hunting in Germany works best when you start networking and applying in your final semester, not after you graduate.
