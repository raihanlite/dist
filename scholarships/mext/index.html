MEXT Scholarship Japan for Bangladeshi Students

19 Nov 2026 · 7 min read · Last checked 19 Nov 2026

The MEXT Scholarship — the Japanese Government (Monbukagakusho) Scholarship — is Japan's flagship fully funded award for international students. It pays your tuition, a monthly allowance and your airfare. For Bangladeshi students it is the main fully funded route to Japan, and the most competitive one. This guide covers the two tracks most relevant to Bangladesh: undergraduate and research (master's and PhD).

Who can apply from Bangladesh

You must be a Bangladeshi citizen (MEXT is open to citizens of countries that have diplomatic relations with Japan), and you apply through the Embassy of Japan in Dhaka. There is no separate consulate route — every Bangladeshi applicant goes through Dhaka. Compare IELTS and TOEFL to choose the right test for you.

  • Undergraduate: you must have completed 12 years of school education (HSC) by the time you arrive, and be under 25 years old.
  • Research: you must hold, or expect to hold, a bachelor's degree (for a master's) or a master's degree (for a PhD) by arrival, and be under 35 years old.
  • Health: you must be in good physical and mental health, as certified in the medical form.
  • Language: there is no IELTS or Japanese-language certificate requirement for the embassy route. The written exam tests English (and Japanese if you choose it), and the preparatory year teaches you Japanese before your degree starts.
  • Grades: MEXT publishes no fixed GPA minimum. Selection is competitive and based on your academic record, the written exam and the interview — strong grades matter, but there is no published cut-off score.

What MEXT covers — and what it does not

The award is genuinely full funding: tuition is waived in full, you receive a monthly allowance for living costs, and MEXT pays your economy airfare to Japan and back. There is no separate tuition bill to worry about.

What you should still plan for:

  • Your first month. The first allowance payment can take a few weeks after you arrive, so bring savings to cover your first month's food, transport and deposits.
  • Accommodation. Many universities offer dormitories, but rent is paid from your allowance. Costs vary a lot between Tokyo and smaller cities.
  • Family. The allowance is for you alone. It is not increased if family members come with you, and bringing dependants on a MEXT scholarship is difficult — plan to go alone.

Two routes, one scholarship: the embassy route and the university route lead to the same award. The embassy route is the only option for undergraduates, and the best option if you have not yet chosen a university or a research supervisor. The university route suits research students who already have a professor willing to supervise them — you apply to the university, and the university recommends you to MEXT.

How to apply: the embassy route, step by step

For Bangladesh, applications go through the Ministry of Education, Bangladesh, when the Embassy of Japan in Dhaka announces the call — usually in April. The 2027 round opened on 29 April 2026 and closed on 14 May 2026. The next round is expected around April 2027, but the exact dates are announced each year and must be confirmed on the embassy's website. Write your research plan like a statement of purpose — specific and focused on one problem.

  1. Watch for the call (March–April). The Embassy of Japan in Dhaka and the Ministry of Education publish the call on their websites. Prepare your documents before it opens — the window is short, usually about two weeks.
  2. Submit your application. Complete the MEXT application form, your research plan or field of study (research track), transcripts, certificates, recommendation letter and medical certificate, and submit them as the call instructs.
  3. Written exam (June–July). Shortlisted candidates sit a written exam at the embassy — English and, for undergraduates, mathematics and science subjects depending on your field. Research applicants sit English and a field-specific paper.
  4. Interview (July–August). Candidates who pass the exam are interviewed at the embassy. Expect questions about why Japan, why your field, and your research plan.
  5. University placement (November–January). MEXT in Tokyo places successful candidates at a Japanese university. You do not choose the university yourself on the embassy route, though you can list preferences.
  6. Final result and arrival (April). Final results come around February–March, and you arrive in Japan in April, when the academic year starts.

Plan the whole process with the application timeline.

How to apply: the university route (research only)

If you already know which professor you want to work with, contact them directly with your CV and research proposal. If they agree to supervise you, the university nominates you to MEXT. University deadlines usually fall between October and December, and each university publishes its own dates. You cannot apply to MEXT directly on this route — the university must recommend you.

Documents checklist

  • MEXT application form (the current year's version — forms change yearly)
  • Field of study and research plan (research track) or placement preference form (undergraduate)
  • Academic transcripts and certificates (SSC, HSC, bachelor's / master's as applicable), attested
  • Recommendation letter from your university teacher or employer
  • Medical certificate on the MEXT form
  • Passport copy (valid well beyond your planned departure)
  • Abstract of your thesis, if you have written one (research track)
  • Photographs as specified in the call

Common mistakes

  • Missing the window. The embassy call is open for about two weeks in April–May. If you start preparing when it opens, you are already late — prepare your documents in March.
  • A vague research plan. For the research track, a generic plan ("I want to study AI in Japan") is the most common reason for rejection. Name a specific problem, a method, and why it needs to be done in Japan.
  • Applying to the wrong route. Undergraduates cannot use the university route. Research students who apply to both routes in the same year can be disqualified — pick one.
  • Using last year's forms. MEXT updates its application forms every year. Download the current year's forms from the embassy or MEXT website.
  • Budgeting the allowance as a salary. ¥117,000–¥145,000 a month covers a student life in Japan, but Tokyo rents and the first month's setup costs are real. Arrive with savings.

After MEXT: what happens when the scholarship ends

A MEXT scholarship ends when your programme ends. It does not automatically lead to a job in Japan or to permanent residence, and you should plan your next step before your final year. Research students who want to stay can look for work in Japan — graduates of Japanese universities can apply for a work visa if they find an employer to sponsor them, and the job-hunting period after graduation is something to discuss with your university's career office early, not in your last month. Others return home with a Japanese degree, Japanese language skills and a network of contacts, which is a strong combination for jobs in Bangladesh's growing number of Japanese companies and development projects. Either way, the value of MEXT is not only the funded degree — it is the years you spend inside Japan's academic and professional world, and the language and connections you take with you.
