Canada Study Permit from Bangladesh — DLI, PAL, Fees and Proof of Funds

19 Nov 2026 · 7 min read · Last checked 19 Nov 2026

Canada's study permit is the visa that lets you study at a designated learning institution (DLI). For a Bangladeshi applicant, the route is: get accepted by a DLI, obtain a provincial attestation letter (PAL), prepare proof of funds, apply online to IRCC, give biometrics at a Visa Application Centre in Dhaka, and wait for a decision. There is no interview in most cases — the decision is made on your documents.

The steps, in order

1. Get accepted by a DLI

Apply to a college or university that is a designated learning institution — only DLIs can host international students. Check the DLI list on canada.ca before you apply, and confirm that your programme is PGWP-eligible if you plan to work in Canada after graduation. Not every DLI programme qualifies for a post-graduation work permit.

2. Get your provincial attestation letter (PAL)

Most study permit applicants now need a PAL from the province or territory where their school is located. Your school normally requests it for you after you accept the offer and pay a deposit. A PAL issued in 2026 is valid until 31 December 2026. Master's and doctoral degree students are exempt from the PAL requirement. If you are applying for Quebec, you need a Quebec Acceptance Certificate (CAQ) instead.

3. Prepare proof of funds

IRCC requires proof that you can pay your first year's tuition, one year of living costs, and travel to and from Canada. For applications made on or after 1 September 2026, a single applicant studying outside Quebec must show CAD $23,448 for living costs, plus first-year tuition and travel. The amount rises with each family member (CAD $29,192 for two people). IRCC updates the figure every September.

The most common way to show the money is a Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC) from a participating Canadian bank, plus proof of tuition paid. IRCC also accepts bank statements (normally the past six months, showing the source of deposits), a bank draft, proof of a paid tuition and housing, an education loan, or a sponsor's letter with supporting documents. Whatever you use, the source of the money must be clear.

4. Apply online to IRCC

Apply online through your IRCC account. You will need your letter of acceptance, PAL, proof of funds, passport, photos, and — depending on your case — a medical exam and a police certificate. Pay the CAD $150 study permit fee and the CAD $85 biometrics fee when you submit. The Student Direct Stream (SDS), the old fast-track route, closed on 8 November 2024 — everyone now applies through the regular stream.

5. Give biometrics in Dhaka

After submitting, you receive a biometrics instruction letter. Book an appointment at the Visa Application Centre in Dhaka (run by VFS Global) to give fingerprints and a photograph. Biometrics are valid for ten years. Most applicants aged 14 to 79 must provide them.

6. Wait for the decision

Processing times vary — check the IRCC processing-time tool for Bangladesh before you plan travel. If approved, you receive a letter of introduction (port of entry letter) and, if needed, a visitor visa or eTA in your passport. The study permit itself is issued when you arrive in Canada — carry your letter of acceptance, PAL, and proof of funds in your hand luggage, not in checked baggage.

There is also a national cap: for 2026, IRCC expects to issue up to about 408,000 study permits, including extensions. The cap is why the PAL exists — provinces receive allocations, and once a province's allocation is used, further PALs are not issued. Apply early in the year if you can.

Financial proof: what counts

  • GIC — a Guaranteed Investment Certificate from a participating Canadian bank, in your name. This is the simplest proof for most Bangladeshi applicants.
  • Proof of tuition paid — a receipt from your DLI for the first year's tuition, or a deposit.
  • Bank statements — the past six months, including the month you apply, showing where deposits came from.
  • Education loan — a sanction letter from a bank.
  • Sponsor's letter — from a parent or relative, with their bank statements and proof of income.
  • Scholarship letter — from a government, university, or recognised body.

IRCC wants to see that the money is genuinely yours or your sponsor's, and that it will still be there when you land. Sudden large deposits with no explanation are the most common reason proof of funds is rejected.

Working and staying after your course

Study permit holders may work up to 24 hours a week off campus during term time, and full time during scheduled breaks. After graduation, the Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) lets you work for almost any employer for up to three years. The PGWP costs CAD $255 ($155 work permit fee + $100 open work permit holder fee). You must apply within 180 days of your school confirming you completed your programme.

Since 1 November 2024, most PGWP applicants must also prove their language level: CLB 7 in English (or NCLC 7 in French) in all four skills for university graduates, CLB 5 for college and other non-university graduates. Master's graduates get a three-year PGWP even if their programme lasted under two years (since 15 February 2024), as long as it was at least eight months. For non-degree programmes, your field of study must be on IRCC's eligible list — bachelor's, master's and doctoral degrees are exempt from that list. The eligible-fields list is frozen for 2026.

Documents checklist

  • Valid passport
  • Letter of acceptance from a DLI
  • Provincial attestation letter (PAL), unless exempt — or CAQ for Quebec
  • Proof of funds: GIC, tuition receipt, bank statements, loan or sponsor letter
  • Statement of purpose explaining your study plans
  • Academic transcripts and certificates (SSC, HSC, bachelor's if applicable)
  • English test result (IELTS General or Academic, as your programme requires)
  • Medical exam, if required
  • Police certificate, if required
  • Photos meeting IRCC specifications
  • Biometrics fee and study permit fee receipts
