USA F-1 Student Visa from Bangladesh — Fees, Interview and Financial Proof

19 Nov 2026 · 8 min read · Last checked 19 Nov 2026

The F-1 visa is the student visa for full-time academic study in the United States. For a Bangladeshi student, the route is: get admitted to a US university, receive the Form I-20, pay the SEVIS fee, complete the DS-160, pay the visa fee, and attend an interview at the US Embassy in Dhaka. There is no agent requirement at any step — you can do all of it yourself.

The steps, in order

1. Get admitted and receive your I-20

Apply to SEVP-certified universities and accept an offer. The university then issues your Form I-20, which shows your programme, its cost, and your SEVIS ID. You cannot start the visa process without it. Check the cost figure on your I-20 carefully — that is the amount you must show you can pay.

2. Pay the SEVIS I-901 fee — $350

Pay the $350 SEVIS fee online at FMJfee.com using the SEVIS ID from your I-20. Pay at least three business days before your interview, and print the receipt. The fee is paid once per SEVIS ID, not once per visa attempt — if you are refused and reapply with the same I-20, you do not pay it again.

3. Complete the DS-160

The DS-160 is the online visa application form, completed at the Consular Electronic Application Center (CEAC) via travel.state.gov. Upload a photo that meets the requirements, and save the confirmation page with its barcode — you need it at the interview.

4. Pay the MRV fee — $185 — and book the interview

Pay the $185 visa application (MRV) fee through the official appointment system, then schedule your interview at the US Embassy in Dhaka. The MRV fee is non-refundable, even if the visa is refused. Since September 2025, interview waivers have been sharply limited — plan on attending in person.

5. Attend the interview

Bring your passport, I-20, DS-160 confirmation, SEVIS fee receipt, MRV receipt, admission letter, financial documents, and academic records. Interviews are usually short — a few minutes. The officer decides on the spot in most cases. If approved, your passport is kept and returned with the visa; if further checks are needed, you will be told.

6. Travel

A student visa can be issued up to 365 days before your programme starts, but you may not enter the US more than 30 days before the start date on your I-20. Book flights only after the visa is in your passport.

Budget $535 in government fees before the interview, plus $250 if the Visa Integrity Fee is collected when your visa is issued. There is no standard "appointment fee" — anyone charging you a separate fee to book an interview is not part of the official process.

Financial proof: what the officer wants to see

The I-20 states the full cost of your first year — tuition plus living costs. You must show you can pay that amount, and that you can fund the following years too. The officer is checking two things: that the money is real, and that it will actually be available to you.

  • Bank statements in your name or your sponsor's name, showing the funds have been held for some time — a large deposit made days before the interview raises questions.
  • Sponsor documents — if a parent or relative is paying, a signed sponsorship letter plus proof of their income or business and their bank statements.
  • Scholarship or assistantship letters — a funding offer from the university is the strongest proof of all; bring the original letter.
  • Education loans — a sanction letter from a bank is accepted, but be ready to explain how repayments will work.
  • Income proof — salary slips, tax returns, or business documents showing where the money comes from. Money with no visible source is a problem.

Do not borrow money to show in a bank statement and return it the next day. Officers see this pattern often, and it is one of the fastest ways to be refused.

The interview: what to expect

Most F-1 interviews last only a few minutes. The officer will ask about your study plans, why you chose this university, how you will pay, and what you plan to do after graduation. Answer in your own words — memorised, agent-written answers sound rehearsed and hurt more than they help.

Common questions include: Why this university and not one in Bangladesh? Why this subject? Who is paying, and what do they do? What will you do after your degree? Have you travelled abroad before? Keep answers short, specific and honest. If you do not know something, say so rather than inventing an answer.

Why F-1 visas get refused

Most refusals are under section 214(b) — the officer was not convinced you will return to Bangladesh after your studies. The usual reasons are:

ReasonWhat it looks likeHow to avoid it
Weak ties to BangladeshNo clear reason to return — no family, job, or plans at homeTalk concretely about your plans after graduation: a job, a family business, further study
Unclear financesMoney appeared suddenly, or the source is unexplainedShow funds held over time, with a clear source of income
Vague study plansCannot explain the course, the university, or why the USKnow your programme, your university, and your reasons — in your own words
Inconsistent answersWhat you say does not match your documentsMake sure your DS-160, I-20 and answers agree
Previous visa historyEarlier refusals or overstays, undisclosedBe honest about past applications — the officer can see them

A refusal is not a ban. You can reapply, but only after fixing the reason for the refusal — reapplying with the same file usually gets the same result.

Working while you study

F-1 students may work on campus up to 20 hours a week during term time, and full time during holidays. Off-campus work needs authorisation: Curricular Practical Training (CPT) for internships that are part of your course, and Optional Practical Training (OPT) for work in your field after graduation — 12 months, or 36 months in total for STEM degrees (12 + 24-month extension). You cannot work off campus in your first academic year except through CPT.

Rules are changing fast in 2026 — check before you plan. The $250 Visa Integrity Fee is being phased in and is not yet charged at every post. In October 2026 the Department of Homeland Security proposed a $70,000 OPT fee (paid by the university, $30,000 for later recommendations) — this is a proposal only, published in the Federal Register on 8 October 2026, and it is not in effect. A proposed limit on how long F-1 students may stay has also been challenged in court. None of these is final. Confirm the current position on travel.state.gov before you pay anything or make plans that depend on OPT.

Documents checklist

  • Valid passport (at least 6 months beyond your planned stay)
  • Form I-20 from your university, signed
  • DS-160 confirmation page with barcode
  • SEVIS I-901 fee payment receipt ($350)
  • MRV fee payment receipt ($185)
  • Admission letter and any scholarship or assistantship letter
  • Academic transcripts and certificates (SSC, HSC, bachelor's if applicable)
  • English test result (IELTS / TOEFL, as the university requires)
  • Financial proof: bank statements, sponsor letter, income proof, loan sanction letter if any
  • One passport photo meeting the US visa photo requirements
  • Evidence of ties to Bangladesh (family, property, job offer, future plans)
