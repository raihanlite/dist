Australia Student Visa (Subclass 500) from Bangladesh

19 Nov 2026 · 7 min read · Last checked 19 Nov 2026

Australia's Student visa (subclass 500) is for full-time study in a course registered on CRICOS, the national register of courses for overseas students. For a Bangladeshi applicant, the route is: get admitted, receive a Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE), buy Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC), prepare financial proof, lodge the application online in ImmiAccount, and wait for a decision. There is no interview in most cases — the decision is made on your documents and your Genuine Student statement.

The steps, in order

1. Get admitted and receive your CoE

Apply to an Australian university or college offering a CRICOS-registered course. Once you accept the offer and pay the deposit or first tuition instalment the institution asks for, it issues your Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) — an electronic record with a code you enter in your visa application. Without a CoE, you cannot lodge the visa. Compare IELTS and TOEFL to choose the right test for you.

2. Buy Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC)

OSHC is compulsory for the whole length of your visa, and you must buy it before you lodge the application. Your university can usually arrange it, or you can buy it directly from an approved provider. The price depends on the length of your course and whether you include family members — budget roughly AUD $600 a year for a single student, and check the exact premium on the provider's website.

3. Prepare your financial proof

Where evidence is required, you must show you can cover 12 months of living costs (AUD $29,710 for a single student), your first 12 months of course fees, and travel — AUD $2,000 for travel if you apply from outside Australia. Add AUD $10,394 for a partner, AUD $4,449 per dependent child, and AUD $13,502 in school costs per school-age child if they come with you.

Acceptable evidence includes bank statements, a loan from a financial institution, a scholarship letter, or proof of income. The funds must be genuinely available to you — Home Affairs looks at the history of the account, not just the balance on one day.

4. Write your Genuine Student statement

The Genuine Student requirement applies to all applications lodged on or after 23 March 2024. You must show that you genuinely intend to study in Australia, that you understand your course and why you chose it, and that you have realistic plans for after graduation. Be specific: name the course content, explain why this university and not one at home, and describe your career plans in Bangladesh. Generic statements copied from an agent are easy to spot and count against you.

5. Lodge in ImmiAccount and pay

Create an account on ImmiAccount, the Department of Home Affairs online portal, and lodge the subclass 500 application. The base application charge for the main applicant is AUD $2,500 for applications lodged on or after 1 July 2026 (AUD $2,050 for standalone English-language-only courses). Additional applicants cost more — AUD $1,530 for an adult dependant, AUD $500 for a child under 18. Confirm your exact total in the official Visa Pricing Estimator before you pay, because concessions and subsequent-application charges can change it.

6. Health checks and biometrics

Most Bangladeshi applicants need a health examination at an approved panel clinic, and may be asked for biometrics. Home Affairs will tell you what is needed after you lodge. Book the health check promptly — delays here are the most common reason applications stall.

7. Wait for the decision

Processing times vary by course sector and by how complete your application is. A complete application with clear financial evidence and a strong Genuine Student statement is processed faster. If Home Affairs needs more information, it will ask through ImmiAccount — check your account and email regularly, and respond within the deadline given. Plan the whole process with the application timeline.

The visa charge has risen every year recently — AUD $710 before July 2024, $1,600, then $2,000 in July 2025, then $2,500 in July 2026. Older guides will understate it. Always confirm the current charge in the Home Affairs pricing estimator. Compare Australia's total cost with seven other countries in the cost comparison.

Places are limited in 2026. Australia set a National Planning Level of 295,000 new international student enrolments for 2026. Universities manage their own allocations, so popular courses and universities can fill up — apply early, and have a second-choice course in mind.

Financial proof: what counts

  • Bank statements — showing the funds available to you, with a history the case officer can follow.
  • Education loan — a sanction letter from a bank or financial institution.
  • Scholarship letter — from a government, university, or recognised body, stating what it covers.
  • Income proof — salary, business income, or other regular income of you or your sponsor.
  • Proof of tuition paid — reduces the amount you need to show for course fees.

Working and staying after your course

Student visa holders may work up to 48 hours per fortnight while their course is in session, and unlimited hours during breaks. After graduation, the Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485) lets you live and work in Australia — usually two years for a bachelor's degree, with longer grants for some master's and doctoral qualifications and for study in regional areas. Check the current rules on the Home Affairs website before you rely on them, as graduate visa settings have changed several times in recent years.

Documents checklist

  • Valid passport
  • Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) code
  • OSHC policy certificate covering your full stay
  • Genuine Student statement
  • Financial proof: bank statements, loan letter, or scholarship letter
  • English test result (IELTS / TOEFL / PTE, as your course requires)
  • Academic transcripts and certificates (SSC, HSC, bachelor's if applicable)
  • Health examination, if requested
  • Biometrics, if requested
  • Visa application charge receipt (AUD $2,500)
