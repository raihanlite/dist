Germany Student Visa from Bangladesh

19 Nov 2026 · 7 min read · Last checked 19 Nov 2026

Germany is the cheapest major study destination for Bangladeshi students, because most public universities charge no tuition — only a semester contribution of roughly €150–350. The visa itself is also cheap: €75. The real requirement is financial proof: a blocked account holding €11,904 for your first year. For a Bangladeshi applicant, the route is: get admitted, open the blocked account, arrange health insurance, apply through the Consular Services Portal, and attend an appointment at the German Embassy in Dhaka.

The steps, in order

1. Get admitted

Apply to a German university — through uni-assist or directly, depending on the university. You need an admission letter (Zulassungsbescheid) before you can apply for the visa. For English-taught programmes you will usually need IELTS (typically 6.0–6.5) or TOEFL; for German-taught programmes you need German at the level the university asks for (often TestDaF or Goethe B2/C1). Bangladeshi applicants do not need an APS certificate — that requirement applies to applicants from a few other countries, not Bangladesh.

2. Open the blocked account (Sperrkonto)

The blocked account is the standard way to prove you can fund your living costs. You deposit €11,904 before your visa appointment; the money is released to you at €992 a month once you arrive in Germany. It is your own money, not a fee — you spend it on rent, food and transport during your first year.

The most used providers are Fintiba and Expatrio; Deutsche Bank also offers a blocked account. Providers charge their own set-up and monthly fees on top of the deposit — check the provider's current price list before you open the account, because the fees change. (Coracle paused new accounts in August 2025.) The confirmation from the provider is one of the documents you take to your visa appointment, so open the account early — transfers from Bangladesh can take days.

3. Arrange health insurance

You need health insurance before the visa is issued. For the visa application, a travel health insurance policy covering your first weeks is normally accepted. After you arrive and enrol, you switch to German public student health insurance, which costs roughly €120 a month. Some blocked-account providers sell a combined package (blocked account + health insurance) — compare the total price rather than assuming the bundle is cheapest.

4. Apply through the Consular Services Portal

Applications are submitted online through the Federal Foreign Office's Consular Services Portal. You fill in the application, upload your documents, and then attend an in-person appointment at the German Embassy in Dhaka for fingerprints and to hand in originals. Pay the €75 visa fee at the appointment (in local currency); it is non-refundable.

5. Wait for the decision

Processing usually takes six to twelve weeks, and longer in the June–August peak season. Apply as soon as your admission letter arrives — do not wait for the semester to approach. If approved, you receive a national visa valid for your first months in Germany.

6. Register and get your residence permit after arrival

Once in Germany, register your address at the local citizens' office, enrol at your university, and apply for a residence permit at the local Foreigners' Office (Ausländerbehörde) before your visa expires. The first residence permit is usually issued for two years. The fee is around €100 — confirm the exact amount at your local office. You will need to show your blocked account, health insurance, enrolment certificate, and proof of address. Plan the whole process with the application timeline.

The amount is unchanged for 2026, but confirm the figure in force on your application date with the German mission before you transfer money.

Other ways to prove your finances

The blocked account is the most common option, but not the only one. You can also prove your funds with:

  • A scholarship — a DAAD or other public scholarship paying at least €992 a month replaces the deposit entirely, and the visa fee is waived for public-scholarship holders.
  • A formal obligation letter (Verpflichtungserklärung) — a person living in Germany declares they will cover your costs.
  • A German bank guarantee or a blocked account at a German bank — the same principle, different provider.
  • Proof of income or assets — in some cases, your parents' income and assets can be accepted, but this is harder to document from Bangladesh and is rarely the smoothest route.

For most Bangladeshi students, the blocked account is the simplest and most predictable option. If you have a scholarship, bring the award letter — it replaces the blocked account and the fee.

Working and staying after your course

Students may work up to 140 full days or 280 half days a year, alongside their studies. After graduation, you can apply for an 18-month residence permit to look for a job in your field. Once you find qualified employment, you can switch to a work residence permit — and after a few years of working and paying into the system, to a settlement permit. This is one of the clearest post-study routes in Europe, and a major reason Germany is popular with Bangladeshi students.

Documents checklist

  • Valid passport
  • Admission letter from a recognised German university
  • Blocked account confirmation (€11,904) or scholarship award letter
  • Health insurance proof (travel insurance for the visa stage)
  • Language certificate (IELTS/TOEFL for English programmes; TestDaF/Goethe for German programmes)
  • Academic transcripts and certificates (SSC, HSC, bachelor's if applicable)
  • CV and motivation letter
  • Passport photos meeting the requirements
  • Visa fee (€75) — paid at the appointment
  • Proof of accommodation in Germany, if you already have it
