International Mother Language Day paragraph

December 11, 2025 by Mohasin Islam · 6 min read

On this page you will find an International Mother Language Day paragraph in 150, 250 and 300 words for Class 6 to 12, SSC and HSC. The short version is best for junior classes, the longer versions are best for SSC and HSC. A Bangla meaning, important word meanings and exam tips are given at the end.

International Mother Language Day Paragraph in 150 Words

Best for Class 6–7.

International Mother Language Day is observed on 21 February to honour the languages spoken by people around the world. The day also recalls the language movement in Bangladesh, when students and citizens protested in 1952 for the right to use Bangla. Several demonstrators lost their lives, and their sacrifice became a lasting symbol of linguistic rights.

People mark the day by visiting Shaheed Minar, placing flowers, arranging discussions, holding cultural programmes, and reading poetry. The observance reminds us that language carries history, identity, and knowledge. We should respect our mother tongue, develop it through reading and writing, and show equal respect for other languages. Protecting linguistic diversity helps communities live with dignity and understanding. On 17 November 1999, UNESCO proclaimed 21 February as International Mother Language Day, and it has been observed around the world since 2000. This honour came because of the sacrifice of 1952.

International Mother Language Day Paragraph in 250 Words

Best for Class 8–10 and SSC.

International Mother Language Day is observed on 21 February to honour the languages spoken by people around the world. The day also recalls the language movement in Bangladesh, when students and citizens protested in 1952 for the right to use Bangla as a state language. On 21 February 1952, police opened fire on a peaceful student procession in Dhaka. Salam, Barkat, Rafiq, Jabbar, and Shafiur Rahman were killed, and their sacrifice became a lasting symbol of linguistic rights. Their memory is honoured at the Shaheed Minar, the Martyrs' Monument in Dhaka.

Years later, the world recognised this sacrifice. On 17 November 1999, UNESCO proclaimed 21 February as International Mother Language Day, and it has been observed around the world since 2000. People mark the day by visiting Shaheed Minar, placing flowers, arranging discussions, holding cultural programmes, and reading poetry. The observance reminds us that language carries history, identity, and knowledge. We should respect our mother tongue, develop it through reading and writing, and show equal respect for other languages. Protecting linguistic diversity helps communities live with dignity and understanding. In Bangladesh, the day begins in the early hours, when people walk barefoot to the Shaheed Minar with flowers in their hands. They sing 'Amar Bhaiyer Rokte Rangano Ekushey February', the song written in memory of the martyrs, and the whole nation remembers the price paid for Bangla. The day teaches us that a mother tongue is not only a means of communication; it is the voice of a people's history, poetry, and dreams.

International Mother Language Day Paragraph in 300 Words

Best for SSC and HSC.

International Mother Language Day is observed on 21 February to honour the languages spoken by people around the world. The day also recalls the language movement in Bangladesh, when students and citizens protested in 1952 for the right to use Bangla as a state language. At that time, most people of the country spoke Bangla, yet the central government had declared that Urdu alone would be the state language. On 21 February 1952, students of the University of Dhaka defied a ban on public gatherings and marched to demand recognition for Bangla. Near Dhaka Medical College, police opened fire on the peaceful procession. Salam, Barkat, Rafiq, Jabbar, and Shafiur Rahman were killed, and many others were injured. Their sacrifice became a lasting symbol of linguistic rights, and within a few years Bangla was recognised as an official language.

Years later, the world recognised this sacrifice. On 17 November 1999, UNESCO proclaimed 21 February as International Mother Language Day, and it has been observed around the world since 2000. People mark the day by visiting Shaheed Minar, placing flowers, arranging discussions, holding cultural programmes, and reading poetry. The observance reminds us that language carries history, identity, and knowledge. We should respect our mother tongue, develop it through reading and writing, and show equal respect for other languages. Protecting linguistic diversity helps communities live with dignity and understanding. Students who protested in 1952 showed that a people who love their language will also defend their dignity. Today, when we speak Bangla freely, we should remember that this freedom was bought with blood. The best way to honour the martyrs is to use Bangla correctly, to read Bangla books, and to teach our children to love their mother tongue while respecting every other language of the world.

Bangla Meaning of International Mother Language Day Paragraph

আন্তর্জাতিক মাতৃভাষা দিবস অনুচ্ছেদের বাংলা অর্থ

আন্তর্জাতিক মাতৃভাষা দিবস প্রতি বছর ২১ ফেব্রুয়ারি পালিত হয়, বিশ্বের সব মানুষের ভাষাকে সম্মান জানাতে। এই দিনটি বাংলাদেশের ভাষা আন্দোলনকেও স্মরণ করিয়ে দেয়, যখন ১৯৫২ সালে ছাত্র ও সাধারণ মানুষ বাংলাকে রাষ্ট্রভাষা করার দাবিতে আন্দোলন করেছিল। ১৯৫২ সালের ২১ ফেব্রুয়ারি ঢাকায় শান্তিপূর্ণ মিছিলে পুলিশ গুলি চালায়। সালাম, বরকত, রফিক, জব্বার ও শফিউর রহমান শহীদ হন এবং তাঁদের আত্মত্যাগ ভাষার অধিকারের চিরন্তন প্রতীক হয়ে ওঠে।

বহু বছর পর বিশ্ব এই আত্মত্যাগকে স্বীকৃতি দেয়। ১৯৯৯ সালের ১৭ নভেম্বর ইউনেস্কো ২১ ফেব্রুয়ারিকে আন্তর্জাতিক মাতৃভাষা দিবস হিসেবে ঘোষণা করে এবং ২০০০ সাল থেকে সারা বিশ্বে এটি পালিত হচ্ছে। এই দিনে মানুষ শহীদ মিনারে ফুল দেয়, আলোচনা সভা ও সাংস্কৃতিক অনুষ্ঠান করে এবং কবিতা পাঠ করে। ভাষা ইতিহাস, পরিচয় ও জ্ঞান বহন করে — তাই মাতৃভাষাকে সম্মান করা এবং অন্য ভাষাকেও সমান সম্মান দেওয়া আমাদের কর্তব্য।

Exam Tips

  • Choose the version that matches your class: 150 words for Class 6–7, 250 words for Class 8–10 and SSC, 300 words for SSC and HSC.
  • Remember the key dates: 21 February 1952 (language martyrs) and 17 November 1999 (UNESCO proclamation).
  • Learn the names of the five martyrs — Salam, Barkat, Rafiq, Jabbar, and Shafiur Rahman — and spell them correctly.
  • Learn the main points and write them in your own sentences.
  • Revise the paragraph once the night before the exam instead of reading it for the first time in the hall.
