The Life of a Farmer paragraph

November 11, 2025 by Mohasin Islam · 1 min read

On this page you will find a Life of a Farmer paragraph in 150, 250 and 300 words for Class 6 to 12, SSC and HSC. The short version is best for junior classes, the longer versions are best for SSC and HSC. A Bangla meaning, important word meanings and exam tips are given at the end.

The Life of a Farmer Paragraph in 150 Words

Best for Class 6–7.

A farmer grows crops and raises animals that help feed society. In Bangladesh many farmers cultivate rice, wheat, pulses, vegetables, jute, sugarcane, and other produce. Their work begins early, when they prepare soil, sow seeds, manage water, remove weeds, and protect plants from pests.

Farming demands patience and physical effort. A farmer may work under strong sun or heavy rain and wait months before knowing whether the harvest will succeed. Floods, drought, cyclones, disease, and insects can destroy crops. When the harvest is good, the farmer supplies food to markets and earns money for the family; when it fails, debt and hardship may follow. Farmers need fair prices, quality seed, affordable credit, irrigation, and storage. Their contribution is fundamental to national life, so agriculture deserves sustained public support. Better roads and markets can reduce waste between field and consumer, and crop insurance can protect farmers from a bad season.

The Life of a Farmer Paragraph in 250 Words

Best for Class 8–10 and SSC.

A farmer grows crops and raises animals that help feed society. In Bangladesh many farmers cultivate rice, wheat, pulses, vegetables, jute, sugarcane, and other produce. Their work begins early, when they prepare soil, sow seeds, manage water, remove weeds, and protect plants from pests. Farming demands patience and physical effort, because the result of months of work is known only at harvest time.

A farmer may work under strong sun or heavy rain and wait months before knowing whether the harvest will succeed. Floods, drought, cyclones, disease, and insects can destroy crops. When the harvest is good, the farmer supplies food to markets and earns money for the family; when it fails, debt and hardship may follow. Many farmers borrow money to buy seed and fertiliser, so a bad season can leave them in debt for years.

Farmers need fair prices, quality seed, affordable credit, irrigation, storage, crop insurance, and access to agricultural advice. Better roads and markets can reduce waste between field and consumer. Respecting farmers also means avoiding food waste and understanding the labour behind every meal. Their contribution is fundamental to national life, so agriculture deserves sustained public support and modern, climate-resilient methods. In recent years, many farmers have started using mobile phones to check market prices and weather forecasts, and this simple technology helps them sell at a better price. Young educated farmers are also trying new methods, such as drip irrigation and organic fertiliser, which save water and keep the soil healthy. If the country supports these efforts, agriculture can become both modern and profitable.

The Life of a Farmer Paragraph in 300 Words

Best for SSC and HSC.

A farmer grows crops and raises animals that help feed society. In Bangladesh many farmers cultivate rice, wheat, pulses, vegetables, jute, sugarcane, and other produce. Their work begins early, when they prepare soil, sow seeds, manage water, remove weeds, and protect plants from pests. Farming demands patience and physical effort, because the result of months of work is known only at harvest time. A farmer cannot hurry a plant; he can only care for it and wait.

A farmer may work under strong sun or heavy rain and wait months before knowing whether the harvest will succeed. Floods, drought, cyclones, disease, and insects can destroy crops. When the harvest is good, the farmer supplies food to markets and earns money for the family; when it fails, debt and hardship may follow. Many farmers borrow money to buy seed and fertiliser, so a bad season can leave them in debt for years. Yet they return to the field each season, because farming is not only their work but also their way of life.

Farmers need fair prices, quality seed, affordable credit, irrigation, storage, crop insurance, and access to agricultural advice. Better roads and markets can reduce waste between field and consumer, so that more of what is grown reaches the people who need it. Respecting farmers also means avoiding food waste and understanding the labour behind every meal. Their contribution is fundamental to national life, so agriculture deserves sustained public support and modern, climate-resilient methods. A nation that forgets its farmers forgets the source of its food. Every meal we eat begins in a farmer's field, and remembering this simple truth is the first step towards respecting farmers. Let us waste less food and thank the hands that grow it. That is the least we can do.

Bangla Meaning of The Life of a Farmer Paragraph

কৃষকের জীবন অনুচ্ছেদের বাংলা অর্থ

কৃষক ফসল ফলায় এবং পশুপালন করে, যা সমাজের খাদ্য জোগায়। বাংলাদেশের অনেক কৃষক ধান, গম, ডাল, শাকসবজি, পাট, আখ ও অন্যান্য ফসল চাষ করে। তাদের কাজ শুরু হয় ভোরে — মাটি তৈরি, বীজ বপন, পানি দেওয়া ও আগাছা পরিষ্কার করা।

চাষাবাদে ধৈর্য ও শারীরিক পরিশ্রম প্রয়োজন। কৃষক প্রখর রোদে বা ভারী বৃষ্টিতে কাজ করে এবং ফসল ভালো হবে কি না, তা জানতে কয়েক মাস অপেক্ষা করে। বন্যা, খরা, ঘূর্ণিঝড়, রোগ ও পোকামাকড় ফসল নষ্ট করতে পারে। ফসল ভালো হলে কৃষক বাজারে খাদ্য সরবরাহ করে ও পরিবারের জন্য আয় করে; ফসল নষ্ট হলে ঋণ ও কষ্ট আসে। কৃষকদের ন্যায্য মূল্য, ভালো বীজ, সহজ ঋণ, সেচ ও সংরক্ষণের ব্যবস্থা প্রয়োজন। তাদের অবদান জাতীয় জীবনের ভিত্তি, তাই কৃষি টেকসই সরকারি সহায়তা পাওয়ার যোগ্য।

Exam Tips

  • Choose the version that matches your class: 150 words for Class 6–7, 250 words for Class 8–10 and SSC, 300 words for SSC and HSC.
  • Learn the main points — the farmer's work, his risks, and what he needs — and write them in your own sentences.
  • Keep one paragraph in one block. Do not break it into many small paragraphs unless the question asks for it.
  • Use the Bangla meaning to understand difficult words first.
  • Revise the paragraph once the night before the exam instead of reading it for the first time in the hall.
