Gender Discrimination Paragraph

February 06, 2026 by Study Koro · 1 min read

On this page you will find a Gender Discrimination paragraph in 150, 250 and 300 words for Class 6 to 12, SSC and HSC. The short version is best for junior classes, the longer versions are best for SSC and HSC. A Bangla meaning, important word meanings and exam tips are given at the end.

Gender Discrimination Paragraph in 150 Words

Best for Class 6–7.

Gender discrimination means treating people differently because they are male or female. It can appear at home, in school, at work, or in public life. For example, a girl may be discouraged from studying, while a boy may be excused from household duties. Such attitudes prevent families and nations from using the abilities of all their citizens.

Everyone deserves equal respect, education, safety, and a fair chance to work. Parents should give sons and daughters the same encouragement, and schools should challenge unfair stereotypes. Laws can protect equal rights, but lasting change also depends on everyday behaviour. When women and men share responsibilities and opportunities, families become stronger and society makes better progress. Teachers should also give girls and boys equal chances in class, in sports, and in leadership roles at school. Equal treatment in small things builds equal respect in big things.

Gender Discrimination Paragraph in 250 Words

Best for Class 8–10 and SSC.

Gender discrimination means treating people differently because they are male or female. It can appear at home, in school, at work, or in public life. For example, a girl may be discouraged from studying, while a boy may be excused from household duties. A woman may be paid less for the same work, or a man may be laughed at for choosing a job that people call a woman's job. Such attitudes prevent families and nations from using the abilities of all their citizens.

Everyone deserves equal respect, education, safety, and a fair chance to work. A girl has the same right as a boy to go to school, to choose a subject she likes, and to dream of a career. Parents should give sons and daughters the same encouragement, and teachers should praise ability, not gender. Schools should challenge unfair stereotypes in books, stories, and everyday talk.

Laws can protect equal rights, but lasting change also depends on everyday behaviour. Boys should learn to share household work, and girls should be given confidence to speak and lead. When women and men share responsibilities and opportunities, families become stronger and society makes better progress. A country cannot move forward if half of its people are held back. In many families, parents still spend more on a son's education than on a daughter's, and this unfair habit must change if society wants real progress. Both sons and daughters can bring honour to a family.

Gender Discrimination Paragraph in 300 Words

Best for SSC and HSC.

Gender discrimination means treating people differently because they are male or female. It can appear at home, in school, at work, or in public life. For example, a girl may be discouraged from studying, while a boy may be excused from household duties. A woman may be paid less for the same work, or a man may be laughed at for choosing a job that people call a woman's job. Girls may be married early instead of finishing their education, and boys may be told that cooking or caring for children is not their work. Such attitudes prevent families and nations from using the abilities of all their citizens.

Everyone deserves equal respect, education, safety, and a fair chance to work. A girl has the same right as a boy to go to school, to choose a subject she likes, and to dream of a career. Parents should give sons and daughters the same encouragement, and teachers should praise ability, not gender. Schools should challenge unfair stereotypes in books, stories, and everyday talk, and they should give girls and boys the same chances in sports, leadership, and science.

Laws can protect equal rights, but lasting change also depends on everyday behaviour. Boys should learn to share household work, and girls should be given confidence to speak and lead. Workplaces should judge people by their skills and hard work, not by their gender. When women and men share responsibilities and opportunities, families become stronger and society makes better progress. A country cannot move forward if half of its people are held back, because talent is not limited to one gender. The media also has a role to play, because films, advertisements, and stories that show women as weak or men as careless only strengthen old stereotypes. Fair stories build a fair society.

Bangla Meaning of Gender Discrimination Paragraph

লিঙ্গ বৈষম্য অনুচ্ছেদের বাংলা অর্থ

লিঙ্গ বৈষম্য বলতে বোঝায় কেউ ছেলে না মেয়ে, সেই কারণে তাকে আলাদা আচরণ করা। এটি বাড়িতে, স্কুলে, কর্মক্ষেত্রে বা জনজীবনে দেখা যায়। যেমন, মেয়েকে পড়াশোনা থেকে নিরুৎসাহিত করা হতে পারে, অথচ ছেলেকে ঘরের কাজ থেকে ছাড় দেওয়া হয়। এ ধরনের মনোভাব পরিবার ও দেশকে সব নাগরিকের যোগ্যতা কাজে লাগাতে বাধা দেয়।

প্রত্যেকেই সমান সম্মান, শিক্ষা, নিরাপত্তা ও কাজের সুযোগ পাওয়ার যোগ্য। বাবা-মায়ের উচিত ছেলে ও মেয়েকে একইভাবে উৎসাহ দেওয়া এবং স্কুলের উচিত অন্যায় ধারণাগুলোকে চ্যালেঞ্জ করা। আইন সমান অধিকার রক্ষা করতে পারে, কিন্তু স্থায়ী পরিবর্তন নির্ভর করে প্রতিদিনের আচরণের ওপর। নারী ও পুরুষ যখন দায়িত্ব ও সুযোগ ভাগ করে নেয়, তখন পরিবার শক্তিশালী হয় এবং সমাজ আরও এগিয়ে যায়।

Exam Tips

  • Choose the version that matches your class: 150 words for Class 6–7, 250 words for Class 8–10 and SSC, 300 words for SSC and HSC.
  • Do not memorise the whole paragraph word by word. Learn the main points — meaning, examples, and how to end it — and write them in your own sentences.
  • Keep one paragraph in one block. Do not break it into many small paragraphs unless the question asks for it.
  • Use the Bangla meaning to understand difficult words first.
  • Revise the paragraph once the night before the exam instead of reading it for the first time in the hall.
