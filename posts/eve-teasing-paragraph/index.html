Eve Teasing Paragraph

February 15, 2026 by Study Koro · 6 min read

On this page you will find an Eve Teasing paragraph in 150, 250 and 300 words for Class 6 to 12, SSC and HSC. The short version is best for junior classes, the longer versions are best for SSC and HSC. A Bangla meaning, important word meanings and exam tips are given at the end.

Eve Teasing Paragraph in 150 Words

Best for Class 6–7.

"Eve-teasing" is a common term for unwanted comments, gestures, following, staring, or other harassment directed at girls and women. It is not harmless fun. It creates fear, restricts movement, interrupts education, and denies a person the right to travel safely.

Families and schools should teach respect, equality, consent, and responsible conduct from an early age. Witnesses can support the victim, seek help from trusted adults or authorities, and avoid blaming the person who was harassed. Communities need safe transport, lighting, complaint systems, and prompt enforcement of the law. Public spaces become safer when every person rejects harassment and protects the dignity of others. Boys and men must understand that silence and peer approval can encourage abuse, and that real respect means speaking up against it. A girl should never be told to change her education, clothing, or dreams to suit someone else's misconduct.

Eve Teasing Paragraph in 250 Words

Best for Class 8–10 and SSC.

"Eve-teasing" is a common term for unwanted comments, gestures, following, staring, or other harassment directed at girls and women. It is not harmless fun. It creates fear, restricts movement, interrupts education, and denies a person the right to travel safely. A girl who faces harassment on the way to school may start missing classes, and some girls even drop out of school because of it. The harm is not only physical; it also damages confidence and peace of mind.

Families and schools should teach respect, equality, consent, and responsible conduct from an early age. Boys must learn that a girl's silence is not permission, and that no one has the right to comment on another person's body or clothes. Witnesses can support the victim, seek help from trusted adults or authorities, and avoid blaming the person who was harassed. A girl should never be told to change her education, clothing, or dreams to accommodate someone else's misconduct.

Communities need safe transport, good lighting, easy complaint systems, and prompt enforcement of the law. Schools and colleges should have clear rules against harassment and a safe way to report it. Public spaces become safer when every person rejects harassment and protects the dignity of others. Schools should also keep a trusted teacher or counsellor whom students can tell about harassment without fear of blame or punishment. When victims are heard and believed, other girls also find the courage to speak. That is how change begins.

Eve Teasing Paragraph in 300 Words

Best for SSC and HSC.

"Eve-teasing" is a common term for unwanted comments, gestures, following, staring, or other harassment directed at girls and women. It is not harmless fun. It creates fear, restricts movement, interrupts education, and denies a person the right to travel safely. A girl who faces harassment on the way to school may start missing classes, and some girls even drop out of school because of it. The harm is not only physical; it also damages confidence and peace of mind for a long time. When half of society cannot move freely, the whole society loses.

Families and schools should teach respect, equality, consent, and responsible conduct from an early age. Boys must learn that a girl's silence is not permission, and that no one has the right to comment on another person's body or clothes. Girls should be taught that harassment is never their fault, and that they have the right to speak up. Witnesses can support the victim, seek help from trusted adults or authorities, and avoid blaming the person who was harassed. Silence and peer approval can encourage abuse, so friends must speak against it instead of laughing along.

Communities need safe transport, good lighting, easy complaint systems, and prompt enforcement of the law. Schools and colleges should have clear rules against harassment and a safe way to report it without fear. A girl should never be told to change her education, clothing, or dreams to accommodate someone else's misconduct. Public spaces become safer when every person rejects harassment and protects the dignity of others. Laws against harassment already exist, but they work only when complaints are taken seriously and punished quickly. The police, the courts, and the community must all do their part so that no harasser feels safe.

Bangla Meaning of Eve Teasing Paragraph

ইভ টিজিং অনুচ্ছেদের বাংলা অর্থ

ইভ টিজিং বলতে মেয়ে ও নারীদের প্রতি অশোভন মন্তব্য, ইঙ্গিত, পিছু নেওয়া, তাকিয়ে থাকা বা অন্য যেকোনো হয়রানিকে বোঝায়। এটি নিরীহ মজা নয়। এটি ভয় সৃষ্টি করে, চলাফেরা সীমিত করে, পড়াশোনায় ব্যাঘাত ঘটায় এবং নিরাপদে চলার অধিকার কেড়ে নেয়।

পরিবার ও স্কুলের উচিত ছোটবেলা থেকেই সম্মান, সমতা, সম্মতি ও দায়িত্বশীল আচরণ শেখানো। প্রত্যক্ষদর্শীরা ভুক্তভোগীকে সাহায্য করতে পারে, বিশ্বস্ত বড়দের বা কর্তৃপক্ষের সাহায্য নিতে পারে এবং ভুক্তভোগীকে দোষারোপ করা থেকে বিরত থাকতে পারে। নিরাপদ যানবাহন, ভালো আলো, সহজ অভিযোগ ব্যবস্থা এবং আইনের দ্রুত প্রয়োগ প্রয়োজন। প্রত্যেকে হয়রানি প্রত্যাখ্যান করলে এবং অন্যের মর্যাদা রক্ষা করলে জনস্থান নিরাপদ হয়।

Exam Tips

  • Choose the version that matches your class: 150 words for Class 6–7, 250 words for Class 8–10 and SSC, 300 words for SSC and HSC.
  • Do not memorise the whole paragraph word by word. Learn the main points — meaning, harm, and what families, witnesses and communities should do — and write them in your own sentences.
  • Keep one paragraph in one block. Do not break it into many small paragraphs unless the question asks for it.
  • Use the Bangla meaning to understand difficult words first.
  • Revise the paragraph once the night before the exam instead of reading it for the first time in the hall.
