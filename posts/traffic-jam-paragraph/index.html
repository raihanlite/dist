Traffic Jam Paragraph for class 6,7,8, SSC

November 25, 2024 by Mohasin Islam · 6 min read

On this page you will find traffic jam paragraph in 150, 250 and 300 words for Class 6 to 12, SSC and HSC. The short version is best for junior classes, and the longer versions are best for SSC and HSC. A Bangla meaning, important word meanings and exam tips are given at the end.

Traffic Jam Paragraph in 150 Words

Best for Class 6–7.

A traffic jam is a long queue of vehicles moving slowly or not at all. It is common in crowded cities where the number of cars, buses, trucks, rickshaws and motorcycles has grown faster than the roads. Dhaka is one of the cities where traffic jams are a daily problem. Narrow streets, road repairs, illegal parking and careless driving make it worse.

A jam wastes valuable time and fuel. Students may miss classes, workers arrive late, and patients can face dangerous delays. Exhaust fumes increase air pollution, while constant horns create stress and noise. Goods reach shops more slowly, which raises business costs.

Better public transport can reduce the number of private vehicles. Authorities should improve signals, create safe footpaths and parking areas, and enforce traffic rules. Traffic police also need good training and equipment. Citizens should plan their journeys and follow directions patiently. Cooperation can make city travel safer and faster.

Traffic Jam Paragraph in 250 Words

Best for Class 8–10 and SSC.

A traffic jam is a long queue of vehicles moving slowly or not at all. It is common in crowded cities where the number of cars, buses, trucks, rickshaws and motorcycles has grown faster than the roads. Dhaka is one of the cities where traffic jams are a daily problem. Every morning and evening, the main roads of Dhaka fill with slow-moving traffic. Narrow streets, road repairs, illegal parking, roadside markets, poor signals and careless driving make congestion worse.

A jam wastes valuable time and fuel. Students may miss classes, workers arrive late, and patients can face dangerous delays. Exhaust fumes increase air pollution, while constant horns create stress and noise. Goods reach shops more slowly, which raises business costs. People waiting in a jam feel tired, angry and helpless, and this harms their health and mood. A single jam can delay thousands of people and waste hours that could be used for study or work.

Better public transport can reduce the number of private vehicles. Authorities should improve signals, create safe footpaths and parking areas, remove illegal obstructions and enforce lane and stopping rules. Traffic police also need good training and equipment. Citizens should plan their journeys, avoid blocking intersections and follow directions patiently. Schools should also teach children road rules so that the next generation drives and walks safely. Long-term city planning that places homes, workplaces and services near reliable transport is essential. Cooperation between planners, drivers, pedestrians and businesses can make city travel safer and faster.

Traffic Jam Paragraph in 300 Words

Best for SSC and HSC.

A traffic jam is a long queue of vehicles moving slowly or not at all. It is common in crowded cities where the number of cars, buses, trucks, rickshaws and motorcycles has grown faster than the roads. Dhaka is one of the cities where traffic jams are a daily problem. Every morning and evening, the main roads of Dhaka fill with slow-moving traffic. When too many vehicles try to use a narrow road at the same time, the result is a jam. Narrow streets, road repairs, illegal parking, roadside markets, poor signals and careless driving make congestion worse.

A jam wastes valuable time and fuel. Students may miss classes, workers arrive late, and patients can face dangerous delays. Exhaust fumes increase air pollution, while constant horns create stress and noise. Goods reach shops more slowly, which raises business costs. People waiting in a jam feel tired, angry and helpless, and this harms their health and mood. Ambulances stuck in traffic cannot reach hospitals in time, and that can cost a life. A single jam can delay thousands of people and waste hours that could be used for study or work.

Better public transport can reduce the number of private vehicles. Authorities should improve signals, create safe footpaths and parking areas, remove illegal obstructions and enforce lane and stopping rules. Traffic police also need good training and equipment. Citizens should plan their journeys, avoid blocking intersections and follow directions patiently. Schools should also teach children road rules so that the next generation drives and walks safely. Long-term city planning that places homes, workplaces and services near reliable transport is essential. Cooperation between planners, drivers, pedestrians and businesses can make city travel safer and faster. A city that moves well is a city that works well, and every citizen has a part to play in it.

Bangla Meaning of Traffic Jam Paragraph

যানজট অনুচ্ছেদের বাংলা অর্থ

যানজট মানে যানবাহনের দীর্ঘ সারি ধীরে চলা বা একেবারে থেমে যাওয়া। জনবহুল শহরে গাড়ি, বাস, ট্রাক, রিকশা ও মোটরসাইকেলের সংখ্যা রাস্তার চেয়ে দ্রুত বেড়েছে। ঢাকায় যানজট প্রতিদিনের সমস্যা।

যানজট মূল্যবান সময় ও জ্বালানি নষ্ট করে। ছাত্ররা ক্লাস মিস করে, কর্মীরা দেরিতে পৌঁছায়, আর রোগীরা বিপজ্জনক দেরির মুখে পড়ে। গাড়ির ধোঁয়া বায়ুদূষণ বাড়ায়।

ভালো গণপরিবহন ব্যক্তিগত গাড়ির সংখ্যা কমাতে পারে। কর্তৃপক্ষের উচিত সংকেত উন্নত করা, নিরাপদ ফুটপাত ও পার্কিংয়ের ব্যবস্থা করা এবং ট্রাফিক নিয়ম কার্যকর করা। নাগরিকদেরও ধৈর্য ধরে নিয়ম মানা উচিত। সবার সহযোগিতায় শহরের যাতায়াত নিরাপদ ও দ্রুত হতে পারে।

Exam Tips

  • Choose the version that matches your class: 150 words for Class 6–7, 250 words for Class 8–10 and SSC, 300 words for SSC and HSC.
  • Do not memorise the whole paragraph word by word. Learn the main points — causes of jams, their effects, and solutions — and write them in your own sentences.
  • Give both causes and solutions. A paragraph with only problems and no solution looks incomplete.
  • Use the Bangla meaning to understand difficult words before you memorise the English version.
  • Revise the paragraph once the night before the exam instead of reading it for the first time in the hall.
