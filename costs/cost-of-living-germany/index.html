Cost of Living in Germany for Bangladeshi Students

24 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · Last checked 24 Nov 2026

Germany is the cheapest of the big destinations once tuition is out of the picture, because public universities charge no tuition and the visa itself sets your budget: €992 a month in a blocked account. Real spending runs €850–€1,200 a month, depending on the city. This guide breaks down the real monthly cost of living in Germany for international students: rent, food, transport, insurance and the one-off costs of arriving. It is part of the cost of living abroad hub, and the figures here feed the full comparison across eight countries.

Accommodation

Rent is the cost that moves your budget most. Student dormitories run €250–€400 a month including utilities and often internet, but places are limited, so apply early. A room in a shared flat (WG) costs €350–€600 in most cities, €550–€900 in Munich, and €250–€450 in Leipzig or Dresden. Your own flat costs €600 or more, and more than €700 in Munich. Expect a deposit of two to three months' cold rent. Apply for a dormitory place through the Studentenwerk as soon as you have your admission letter: it is the cheapest housing in Germany, and the waiting lists are long. For the full picture on universities, visas and work rules, read the Germany country guide and the Germany student visa guide.

Food

Food is the second-largest expense, and the one you control most. A weekly grocery shop for one person costs €35–€55, which works out to €140–€220 a month. Every German university runs a Mensa (cafeteria) where a hot meal costs €2–€4, the cheapest hot lunch in Europe. Eating out regularly pushes food to €250–€300 a month. Cook at home, shop at ALDI, Lidl or Turkish supermarkets, and use the Mensa on busy days. Halal meat and Bangladeshi groceries are easy to find in Berlin, Hamburg, Frankfurt and Munich, and Turkish supermarkets often beat mainstream prices on those staples.

Transport

Transport is often free at the margin: the semester contribution (about €150–€350 per semester) usually includes a semester ticket for local public transport, and the Deutschlandticket costs €63 a month through December 2026. Beyond the ticket, budget €0–€80 a month, mostly for occasional long-distance trains. A bicycle covers most German university towns for free after the one-off purchase, and it is the normal way students get around. One-off arrival costs

Budget for these before you fly. The blocked account itself is not a cost: you deposit €11,904 and get €992 back each month, but the account setup fee (€70–€150) and transfer costs are real. A rental deposit of two to three months' cold rent, the flight from Dhaka, and basic setup items add roughly €1,500–€2,500 on top of your first month's living costs. The visa fee is €75. The semester contribution (€150–€350) is due when you enrol, and it often includes your transport ticket, so it pays for itself quickly. Note the €992 monthly release is a floor for visa maths, not a promise that Munich rent and insurance fit inside it without careful budgeting.

How to keep costs down

Pick a cheaper city: Leipzig or Dresden saves hundreds a month against Munich, mostly on rent. Get a dormitory place, cook at home, and eat at the Mensa. Students can work 140 full days or 280 half days a year, and at the 2026 minimum wage of €13.90 an hour, 20 hours a week during term earns roughly €1,100 gross a month, enough to cover living costs in most cities. Check whether your state charges tuition: most public universities charge none, but some states and some programmes do, so read your university's fee page. Finally, run your own numbers with the study-abroad cost estimator, using your university's actual costs instead of national averages.

Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need per month to live in Germany as a student?

Most international students spend €850–€1,200 a month, excluding tuition. Cheaper cities sit near €665–€1,105; Munich sits near €1,150–€1,250.

Is €992 a month enough in Germany?

In most cities, yes: it is the blocked-account figure and the DAAD range starts below it. In Munich it is tight, and you will need careful budgeting or a dormitory place.

How much is the blocked account for a German student visa?

€11,904 for 12 months (€992 a month) for studies starting in 2026. The money is yours and is released monthly; only the setup fee is a real cost.

What is the cheapest student city in Germany?

Leipzig, Dresden, Chemnitz and Jena are cheapest, at about €665–€1,105 a month, because shared-flat rooms there cost €250–€450.

Can part-time work cover my living costs in Germany?

Yes, more easily than in most countries. Students may work 140 full days or 280 half days a year, and 20 hours a week during term at the minimum wage covers living costs in most cities.
