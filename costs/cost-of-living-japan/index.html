Cost of Living in Japan for Bangladeshi Students

24 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · Last checked 24 Nov 2026

Japan is the surprise of the eight countries: Tokyo is expensive, but the national average is lower than the UK or the USA, and outside Tokyo it is close to Germany. A student spends about ¥85,000–¥130,000 a month, with Tokyo near ¥130,000 and smaller cities near ¥60,000–¥90,000. This guide breaks down the real monthly cost of living in Japan for international students: rent, food, transport, bills and the one-off costs of arriving. It is part of the cost of living abroad hub, and the figures here feed the full comparison across eight countries.

Accommodation

Rent is the cost that moves your budget most, and it is also the main reason for the gap between Tokyo and other regions. University dormitories are the cheapest option at ¥10,000–¥40,000 a month, and about 70% of international students live in private houses or apartments. Private share houses run ¥30,000–¥60,000 a month and usually include utilities and internet. A small studio in Tokyo typically runs ¥60,000–¥80,000. The JASSO survey puts average monthly housing costs at ¥41,000 nationally and ¥57,000 in Tokyo. Expect move-in costs of ¥200,000–¥300,000 in Tokyo: first and last month's rent, a deposit, an agency fee and a guarantor fee. For the full picture on universities, visas and scholarships, read the Japan country guide and the MEXT scholarship guide.

Food

Japan is surprisingly affordable for food if you eat strategically. University cafeterias serve meals for ¥300–¥600, a bowl of ramen at a chain restaurant costs ¥500–¥900, and a bento box at a convenience store costs ¥400–¥600. Supermarket groceries for home cooking run about ¥20,000–¥25,000 a month. If you mostly cook at home, food stays near ¥25,000–¥30,000 a month, but eating out often can push it past ¥40,000. Halal food and Bangladeshi groceries are available in Tokyo and Osaka, and usually cost more than mainstream supermarkets. Evening discounts at supermarkets (bento and sushi marked down after 7 pm) are a real student habit.

Transport

Japan's public transit is efficient but not cheap, so the student commuter pass is your main cost lever. A single train ride in Tokyo costs ¥140–¥400, while a student commuter pass costs ¥5,000–¥15,000 a month depending on the route. A Tokyo student commuting 25 minutes each way might pay ¥8,000–¥12,000 a month for a three-month pass, against ¥30,000 or more on daily fares. Buy the pass for the longest period you can: three-month and six-month passes cost less per month. Compare the full first-year bill, tuition included, in the cost of studying abroad comparison.

One-off arrival costs

Budget for these before you fly. Move-in costs (deposit, agency fee, guarantor fee and first month's rent) can reach ¥200,000–¥300,000 in Tokyo, far more than the one month's deposit students expect. The flight from Dhaka, bedding and basic household items add more. National university tuition is ¥535,800 a year plus a one-time admission fee of ¥282,000 in the first year, so the first year costs more than later years even before living costs. The MEXT scholarship changes the maths entirely: its monthly stipend covers nearly all living costs even in Tokyo, and it pays tuition too.

How to keep costs down

Live in a dormitory or share house, cook at home, and buy the longest commuter pass you can. Choose a university outside Tokyo if you can: Fukuoka or Sendai saves ¥30,000–¥50,000 a month against Tokyo, mostly on rent. Part-time work is allowed with permission from immigration, and the hourly pay covers food and transport. Use the university cafeteria, buy marked-down bento in the evening, and get a budget SIM instead of a major carrier plan. Finally, run your own numbers with the study-abroad cost estimator, using your university's actual costs instead of national averages.

Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need per month to live in Japan as a student?

Most international students spend ¥85,000–¥130,000 a month, excluding tuition. The national average is about ¥105,000; Tokyo is about ¥130,000, and Fukuoka or Sendai about ¥60,000–¥90,000.

Is ¥100,000 a month enough in Japan?

Outside Tokyo, yes, if you live in a dormitory or share house and cook at home. In Tokyo it is tight: rent alone averages ¥57,000 there.

What is the cheapest student city in Japan?

Fukuoka, Sendai and Sapporo are cheapest, at about ¥57,500–¥99,000 a month, because rents there run ¥25,000–¥40,000 for a shared room.

Does MEXT cover living costs in Japan?

Yes, largely. The MEXT scholarship pays a monthly stipend plus full tuition, which covers nearly all living costs even in Tokyo. Self-funded students need savings or family support.

How much is the deposit for a flat in Japan?

Move-in costs can reach ¥200,000–¥300,000 in Tokyo: first and last month's rent, a deposit, an agency fee and a guarantor fee. Dormitories and share houses charge far less.
