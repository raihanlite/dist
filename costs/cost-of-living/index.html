Cost of Living Abroad from Bangladesh: 8 Countries Compared

24 Nov 2026 · 11 min read · Last checked 24 Nov 2026

Tuition is only half the bill. The other half is rent, food and transport, and it varies far more than tuition does: a student in Malaysia lives on about RM 1,500 a month, while the same life in Sydney costs five times as much. This guide compares the average cost of living across the eight countries Bangladeshi students choose most: the USA, UK, Canada, Australia, Germany, Japan, China and Malaysia. Every figure is a monthly or annual living cost, excluding tuition, with the official proof-of-funds figure beside it where one exists. Each country has its own detailed guide linked below, and the full first-year bill with tuition is in the cost of studying abroad comparison.

How to read these numbers

Three things matter more than the national averages. First, the official proof figure and real spending are different things. The visa asks you to show a minimum: Germany's €992 a month, Canada's CAD $23,448 a year, Australia's AUD $29,710 a year, the UK's £1,570 in London or £1,203 outside. Real spending can run above or below that figure, and in Toronto, London and Sydney it usually runs above it. Second, the city matters more than the country. New York costs twice a Midwestern college town, London costs half as much again as Leeds, and Shanghai costs twice Xi'an. Third, the ranges are independent: a careful student can sit near the low end of every row, and a big-city student near the high end. Use the study-abroad cost estimator with your university's actual costs instead of these averages.

Country by country

USA: $1,200–$2,500 a month

The USA has the widest range of the eight, because the country is the biggest and the cities differ most. A small Midwestern college town costs $1,100–$1,600 a month; New York, San Francisco and Boston cost $2,000–$3,000. Rent is 40–50% of the budget, and sharing a flat is the single biggest saving. Read the full cost of living in the USA guide for city budgets and arrival costs.

UK: £1,200–£1,800 a month

The UK is the most expensive of the mid-range destinations, and the gap is almost entirely London: about £1,770 a month there, against £1,200–£1,400 outside it. Halls include bills, shared houses are cheapest after the first year, and a 16-25 Railcard takes a third off train fares. Read the full cost of living in the UK guide for London and outside-London budgets.

Canada: CAD $1,500–$2,500 a month

Canada sits in the middle, but Montreal costs about CAD $1,100–$1,700 while Toronto costs CAD $1,800–$2,500. Rent is 40–50% of the budget, and sharing a two-bedroom flat is the norm. Winter adds heating bills and a one-off winter wardrobe. Read the full cost of living in Canada guide for city budgets and the proof-of-funds rules.

Australia: AUD $1,450–$3,330 a month

Australia looks the most expensive on paper, but the range is wide: Adelaide costs AUD $1,430–$2,560 a month, while Sydney costs AUD $1,690–$3,030. Rent takes about 60% of the budget, and it is quoted weekly, so check the monthly maths. Transport rules differ by state: Brisbane trips cost 50 cents, Sydney has no concession for most international students. Read the full cost of living in Australia guide for city budgets and concession rules.

Germany: €850–€1,200 a month

Germany is the cheapest of the big destinations, and the only one where the visa sets your budget for you: €992 a month in a blocked account. Public universities charge no tuition, dormitories cost €250–€400, Mensa meals cost €2–€4, and the semester ticket usually covers transport. Munich is the exception: budget €1,150–€1,250 there. Read the full cost of living in Germany guide for city budgets and the blocked-account rules.

Japan: ¥85,000–¥130,000 a month

Japan is the surprise: the national average is about ¥105,000 a month, lower than the UK or the USA, and outside Tokyo it falls to ¥60,000–¥90,000. Rent is the reason Tokyo costs more, and the student commuter pass is the main cost lever. The MEXT scholarship changes the maths entirely, covering nearly all living costs even in Tokyo. Read the full cost of living in Japan guide for city budgets and move-in costs.

China: ¥2,000–¥6,000 a month

China is the second-cheapest, and the cheapest with a dormitory bed: many students live on ¥2,000–¥3,500 a month in a Tier 2 city. The city tier is everything: Shanghai costs twice Xi'an. Canteen meals cost ¥6–¥15, transport costs almost nothing, and the CSC scholarship stipend alone covers Tier 2 living costs in full. Read the full cost of living in China guide for city tiers and the CSC stipend.

Malaysia: RM 1,500–RM 2,500 a month

Malaysia is the cheapest of the eight, and the most forgiving: food is cheap, English works everywhere, and a month costs RM 1,500–RM 2,500 in Kuala Lumpur, less elsewhere. Hawker meals cost RM 8–RM 15, so eating out can cost less than cooking, and the student transport pass is RM 100 a month. Read the full cost of living in Malaysia guide for city budgets and arrival costs.

Budgeting for the first month

Your first month always costs more than the rest. Every country has the same one-off costs: a rental deposit (one month's rent in the USA and UK, up to five weeks' rent in the UK, four weeks' rent in Australia, two to three months' cold rent in Germany, up to ¥300,000 in Tokyo move-in costs), the flight from Dhaka, warm clothing for cold countries, and bedding and kitchen basics. Add roughly one extra month's living costs to your first-month budget for these. Then set your monthly budget from the table above, not from the visa minimum: the visa figure is a floor, and real spending in London, Toronto and Sydney runs above it. Hold 5–10% more than the required figure, and hold it for the full required period the visa asks for.

How to keep living costs down

Share a flat, cook at home, and pick your city with the same care you pick your university: a rent gap of a few hundred a month matters more over a degree than a small tuition gap. Take the dormitory bed where one is offered: it is the cheapest housing in Germany, China and Japan. Learn the transport pass on day one: the semester ticket in Germany, the 16-25 Railcard in the UK, the U-Pass in Canada, the 50-cent trips in Brisbane. Part-time work helps with food and transport everywhere, and in Germany it can cover living costs entirely, but it rarely covers rent on its own. Finally, check the scholarship guides: a full award beats every budgeting trick on this page.

Frequently asked questions

Which country is cheapest to live in as a student?

Malaysia is cheapest, at about RM 1,500–RM 2,500 a month in Kuala Lumpur, then China in a Tier 2 city with a dormitory bed, then Germany. Australia and the UK are the most expensive overall.

How much money do I need per month to live abroad as a student?

It depends on the country and the city: roughly ৳37,000–76,000 a month in Malaysia or China, ৳1.2–1.6 lakh in Germany, and ৳1.5–3.1 lakh in the USA, UK or Australia. Big cities cost far more than small ones in the same country.

Do these living costs include tuition?

No. Every figure on this page is living costs only: rent, food, transport, bills and personal costs. Tuition is separate, and the full first-year bill with tuition is in the cost of studying abroad comparison.

How much money must I show for a student visa?

Germany asks for €11,904 a year in a blocked account, Canada CAD $23,448 a year, Australia AUD $29,710 a year, and the UK £1,570 a month in London or £1,203 outside. The USA uses your university's I-20 estimate. Japan, China and Malaysia have no single national figure.

Can part-time work cover my living costs abroad?

Partly, in most countries: it typically covers food and transport. Germany is the exception, where 20 hours a week at the minimum wage can cover living costs in most cities. Rent usually needs savings, family support or a scholarship.

Is it cheaper to live on campus or off campus?

On campus is usually cheapest where dormitories exist: Germany, China and Japan. In the UK, halls include bills and suit the first year, then a shared house is cheaper. In the USA and Canada, sharing a flat off campus usually beats room and board.

How do I budget for the first month abroad?

Add roughly one extra month's living costs to your first-month budget for the deposit, flights, clothing and setup. Your first month in the UK, for example, realistically costs about £1,800 outside London and £2,750 in London once the deposit is included.
