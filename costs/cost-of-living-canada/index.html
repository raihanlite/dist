Cost of Living in Canada for Bangladeshi Students

24 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · Last checked 24 Nov 2026

Canada sits in the middle of the eight countries on living costs, but the spread between cities is wide: Montreal costs about CAD $1,100–$1,700 a month, while Toronto costs CAD $1,800–$2,500. This guide breaks down the real monthly cost of living in Canada for international students: rent, food, transport, bills and the one-off costs of arriving. It is part of the cost of living abroad hub, and the figures here feed the full comparison across eight countries.

Accommodation

Rent is the cost that moves your budget most. On-campus residence averages CAD $800–$1,400 a month, and meals are often included in residence plans, which cuts the food bill. Off campus, a room in a shared two-bedroom flat costs $600–$900 in mid-sized cities such as Halifax, Ottawa and Calgary, $800–$1,200 in Toronto and $750–$1,100 in Vancouver. A one-bedroom flat on your own runs $2,100–$2,600 in Toronto and about $1,600 in Edmonton. Sharing is the norm: your share of a two-bedroom flat can drop to $800–$1,300 a month. Expect to pay first and last month's rent as a deposit ($1,200–$3,600). For the full picture on universities, visas and work rules, read the Canada country guide and the Canada study permit guide.

Food

If you cook most meals at home, groceries run CAD $250–$350 a month. University meal plans cost $400–$600, convenient but pricier than cooking. A casual restaurant meal costs $20–$30, and a fast-food combo runs $13–$17, so most students land at $400–$500 a month for all food by cooking during the week and eating out occasionally. A basic basket of milk, bread, rice, eggs, chicken, potatoes and onions costs about CAD $40. Halal meat and Bangladeshi groceries are easy to find in Toronto, Vancouver and Montreal, and South Asian grocery shops often beat supermarket prices on rice, lentils and spices.

Transport

Most Canadian cities offer student transit passes at steep discounts. Toronto's post-secondary monthly pass costs about CAD $128, Vancouver's U-Pass runs around $47, and smaller cities often charge just $20–$35 a month. A car is the expensive option: insurance alone costs $1,200–$2,000 a year, plus fuel and maintenance, so most international students stick with public transit. Winter changes the maths a little: heating bills rise, and a good winter coat and boots are a one-off cost, not a monthly one. Compare the full first-year bill, tuition included, in the cost of studying abroad comparison.

One-off arrival costs

Budget for these before you fly. First and last month's rent as a deposit ($1,200–$3,600), the flight from Dhaka, and winter gear if you land before winter ($200–$400) add roughly CAD $2,000–$4,500 on top of your first month's living costs. The study permit costs CAD $150 plus $85 biometrics. The proof-of-funds figure (CAD $23,448 a year from 1 Sep 2026) is the minimum you must show for the visa, not necessarily what you will spend: Toronto students spend above it, Montreal students can spend below it.

How to keep costs down

Pick your city with the same care you pick your university: Montreal or Halifax saves hundreds a month against Toronto, mostly on rent. Share a flat, cook at home, and get the student transit pass (or U-Pass) on day one. Students can work up to 24 hours a week off campus during term, which typically covers food and transport. Buy winter clothing in Canada rather than Dhaka: it is made for the climate and often cheaper in end-of-season sales. Finally, run your own numbers with the study-abroad cost estimator, using your university's actual costs instead of national averages.

Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need per month to live in Canada as a student?

Most international students spend CAD $1,500–$2,500 a month, excluding tuition. Montreal and Halifax sit near $1,100–$1,700; Toronto and Vancouver sit near $1,600–$2,500.

Is CAD $1,500 a month enough in Canada?

In Montreal, Halifax or Calgary, yes, if you share a flat and cook at home. In Toronto or Vancouver it is tight: rent alone can take $900–$1,200.

How much money must I show for a Canada study permit?

CAD $23,448 a year for living costs (about $1,954 a month), from 1 Sep 2026, outside Quebec, plus your first year of tuition and travel costs. Quebec has its own figures.

What is the cheapest student city in Canada?

Montreal is the cheapest big student city, at about CAD $1,100–$1,700 a month. Smaller cities such as Halifax are similar, and rents there are lower still.

Can part-time work cover my living costs in Canada?

Partly. Up to 24 hours a week off campus during term typically covers food and transport. Rent needs savings, family support or a scholarship.
