Cost of Living in the USA for Bangladeshi Students

24 Nov 2026 · 7 min read · Last checked 24 Nov 2026

Tuition gets the headlines, but living costs decide whether your US budget works. A student in a small Midwestern college town can live on about $1,200 a month, while the same life in New York or San Francisco costs more than twice that. This guide breaks down the real monthly cost of living in the USA for international students: rent, food, transport, bills and the one-off costs of arriving. It is part of the cost of living abroad hub, and the figures here feed the full comparison across eight countries.

Accommodation

Rent is the cost that moves your budget most. On-campus room and board typically runs $10,000–$16,000 for a full academic year, paid as one lump sum through the university. Off campus, a room in a shared flat averages $700–$1,200 a month in most college towns. A one-bedroom flat on your own can cost $600 in a rural area and $3,000 or more in Boston. Expect to pay a deposit of one month's rent, sometimes first and last month together, before you get the keys. Sharing a two-bedroom flat with three or four students is the most common way Bangladeshi students cut this cost. For the full picture on universities, visas and work rules, read the USA country guide and the USA F-1 visa guide.

Food

A student who cooks most meals at home spends $250–$400 a month on groceries, which lines up with the US Department of Agriculture's moderate-cost food plan for young adults. University meal plans cost $2,000–$5,000 for a full academic year, and regular takeout pushes the food bill to $500–$700 a month. Cooking in bulk with flatmates, shopping at Walmart, ALDI or Costco, and using the campus dining hall only on busy days keeps this line under control. Halal meat and Bangladeshi groceries are easy to find in most college towns with a South Asian community, and usually cost a little more than supermarket own-brand meat.

Transport

Most students rely on a monthly transit pass, which costs $40–$100 depending on the city. New York's MTA and Boston's MBTA passes sit at the top of that range, around $75–$130. Many campuses run free shuttle buses, and a bicycle covers most college towns for free after the one-off purchase. A car is the expensive option: insurance, fuel and parking add hundreds a month, and most international students go without one for at least the first year. Compare the full first-year bill, tuition included, in the cost of studying abroad comparison.

One-off arrival costs

Budget for these before you fly. A rental deposit (one month's rent, sometimes two), the flight from Dhaka (about $1,200 return), warm clothing if you land in winter ($200–$400), and basic setup items for your room ($100–$300) add up to roughly $2,000–$3,500 on top of your first month's living costs. The visa itself costs $535 in SEVIS and MRV fees, plus the $250 Visa Integrity Fee where it applies. Health insurance is usually billed per semester through the university, so check whether your first bill lands in week one.

How to keep costs down

Share a flat, cook at home, and pick your city with the same care you pick your university: a $20,000 tuition difference between two universities matters less than a $1,000 monthly rent difference over four years. On-campus jobs (up to 20 hours a week during term on an F-1 visa) typically pay $10–$15 an hour and cover groceries and transport, not rent. Buy a used bicycle, use the campus shuttle, and check whether your university's health plan can be waived for a cheaper compliant plan. Finally, run your own numbers with the study-abroad cost estimator, using your university's actual I-20 figures instead of national averages.

Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need per month to live in the USA as a student?

Most international students spend $1,200–$2,500 a month, excluding tuition. Small college towns sit near $1,100–$1,600; New York, San Francisco and Boston sit near $2,000–$3,000.

Is $1,500 a month enough in the USA?

In a Midwestern or Southern college town, yes, if you share a flat and cook at home. In New York, Boston or San Francisco, $1,500 barely covers rent, so it is not enough there.

What is the cheapest way to live as a student in the USA?

Share a flat with other students, cook at home, use campus transport, and choose a university in a smaller city. On-campus room and board is simple but rarely the cheapest option.

Does the I-20 living-cost figure match real spending?

It is a planning figure, not a promise. Universities estimate about $14,400 a year for living costs, but real spending in an expensive city runs well above it, and in a cheap college town it can run below it.

Can part-time work cover my living costs in the USA?

Not fully. On-campus work up to 20 hours a week during term typically covers food and transport. Rent needs savings, family support or a scholarship.
