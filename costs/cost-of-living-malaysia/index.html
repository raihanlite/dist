Cost of Living in Malaysia for Bangladeshi Students

24 Nov 2026 · 7 min read · Last checked 24 Nov 2026

Malaysia is the cheapest of the eight countries, and the most forgiving for a Bangladeshi student: food is cheap, English works everywhere, and a month costs RM 1,500–RM 2,500 in Kuala Lumpur, less elsewhere. This guide breaks down the real monthly cost of living in Malaysia for international students: rent, food, transport, bills and the one-off costs of arriving. It is part of the cost of living abroad hub, and the figures here feed the full comparison across eight countries.

Accommodation

Accommodation is the single biggest line item, and it varies more than any other cost by city and room type. A room in university accommodation costs RM 300–RM 1,200 a month, cheapest and closest to campus. A room in a shared condo off campus costs RM 600–RM 1,200, or RM 800–RM 1,500 in Kuala Lumpur, and a private studio or one-bedroom runs RM 1,200–RM 2,000 in the capital. Penang and Johor Bahru cut rent meaningfully: RM 300–RM 600 a month below Kuala Lumpur for equivalent housing. Private landlords usually want two months' rent plus a half-month utility deposit, about RM 2,000–RM 4,000 in Kuala Lumpur. For the full picture on universities, visas and student life, read the Malaysia country guide and the application timeline.

Food

Food is where Malaysia genuinely saves you money. A plate of nasi lemak or a bowl of noodle soup at a local mamak or hawker stall typically costs RM 6–RM 12, and university areas are full of these options. Eating out can cost less than cooking: a hawker meal runs RM 8–RM 15, so a mixed diet of hawker food and occasional cooking lands at RM 550–RM 750 a month, while mostly self-cooked eating runs RM 400–RM 550. Mid-range restaurants push food to RM 800–RM 1,100. Halal food is everywhere, and Bangladeshi groceries, rice, lentils and spices are easy to find in Kuala Lumpur and Penang. Compare the full first-year bill, tuition included, in the cost of studying abroad comparison.

Transport

If you live within walking distance of campus, transport costs can be close to zero on most days. Students who rely on public transport in the Klang Valley (LRT, MRT and bus) typically spend RM 100–RM 150 a month, and Kuala Lumpur's unlimited student pass for LRT, MRT, monorail and buses is RM 100 a month. Ride-hailing instead of public transport is what pushes this to the top of the range: Grab rides usually cost RM 1.50–RM 10 depending on distance and time of day. Long-distance buses cost roughly RM 30–RM 45. A second-hand bicycle covers most university towns for free after the one-off purchase.

One-off arrival costs

Budget for these before you fly. A rental deposit of two months' rent plus a half-month utility deposit (RM 2,000–RM 4,000 in Kuala Lumpur), the flight from Dhaka (RM 1,000–RM 4,000 return, depending on season), and basic setup items add roughly RM 3,000–RM 6,000 on top of your first month's living costs. You buy an approved Malaysian health insurance plan for your whole stay (RM 498–RM 644 a year on the 2026 plans) and have a medical check within seven days of arriving. EMGS and student-pass fees vary by nationality, so check the exact figure for a Bangladeshi passport on the EMGS calculator before you budget.

How to keep costs down

Live in a university hostel for the first year, eat at hawker stalls and mamak shops, and use the RM 100 student transport pass. Pick Penang or a smaller university town over Kuala Lumpur if your course allows it: the rent gap alone is hundreds a month. Cook in bulk with housemates, buy a second-hand bicycle, and keep air-conditioning use in check: it is most of the variance in the utilities bill. Part-time work rules for international students are set by the immigration department, so confirm them before you count on work income. Finally, run your own numbers with the study-abroad cost estimator, using your university's actual costs instead of national averages.

Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need per month to live in Malaysia as a student?

Most international students spend RM 1,500–RM 2,500 a month in Kuala Lumpur, excluding tuition, and RM 1,200–RM 2,000 in Penang or Johor Bahru.

Is RM 1,500 a month enough in Malaysia?

In Kuala Lumpur, RM 1,500 is enough if you live in a hostel and eat at hawker stalls. In Penang or a smaller town, RM 1,500 is comfortable.

What is the cheapest student city in Malaysia?

Smaller university towns are cheapest, at about RM 950–RM 1,550 a month. Among the big cities, Penang is cheapest, at about RM 1,200–RM 2,000.

Is food really cheaper than cooking in Malaysia?

Often, yes. A hawker meal costs RM 8–RM 15, so eating out can cost less than cooking, which is unusual. Cooking at home is still cheapest if you buy in bulk.

How much is the student transport pass in Kuala Lumpur?

RM 100 a month for unlimited LRT, MRT, monorail and bus travel. Without the pass, budget RM 100–RM 150 a month on public transport.
