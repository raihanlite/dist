Cost of Living in China for Bangladeshi Students

24 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · Last checked 24 Nov 2026

China is the second-cheapest of the eight countries, and the cheapest where the university gives you a dormitory bed: many students live on ¥2,000–¥3,500 a month in a Tier 2 city. The catch is the city tier: Shanghai costs twice what Xi'an does. This guide breaks down the real monthly cost of living in China for international students: rent, food, transport, bills and the one-off costs of arriving. It is part of the cost of living abroad hub, and the figures here feed the full comparison across eight countries.

Accommodation

Most international students live in on-campus dormitories, the cheapest option and the easiest to arrange. A standard shared dorm room costs ¥250–¥500 a month at most universities, and an upgraded en-suite dorm ¥500–¥1,000. Renting off campus is a different situation: a room in a shared flat starts at around ¥1,500–¥2,000 a month in Shenzhen or Beijing, and a studio in Shanghai can cost ¥3,000–¥5,000. In Tier 2 cities, a shared flat runs ¥1,000–¥2,000. If your university offers a dormitory bed, take it: it is usually the single biggest saving in your whole budget. For the full picture on universities, visas and scholarships, read the China country guide and the CSC scholarship guide.

Food

Food is surprisingly cheap if you eat like a local. The university canteen is your best tool: a full meal costs ¥6–¥15, and eating there twice a day keeps your monthly food bill at ¥400–¥700. Add breakfast and the occasional meal outside, and a realistic canteen-based budget is ¥700–¥1,000 a month. Street food such as jianbing or baozi costs even less. If you cook at home, monthly groceries land around ¥800–¥1,200 in almost any city. The only place where food prices sting is Shanghai: a decent restaurant dinner there easily hits ¥100. Halal food is widely available in cities with Muslim communities, and university canteens in many cities serve halal meals.

Transport

Transport is a small expense for most students. Many walk or cycle everywhere on campus and spend close to nothing. When you do need to travel, China's metro systems are clean, reliable and cheap: a single ride costs ¥2–¥8, and a monthly transport budget of ¥100–¥300 covers most students. A second-hand bicycle costs about ¥200 and makes campus and nearby streets free. Compare the full first-year bill, tuition included, in the cost of studying abroad comparison.

One-off arrival costs

Budget for these before you fly. A dormitory deposit is small, but a private landlord usually wants one to three months' rent upfront. The flight from Dhaka, bedding and basic household items add roughly ¥2,000–¥4,000 on top of your first month's living costs. Health insurance is mandatory and charged at registration, usually ¥800–¥1,200 a year for the university's comprehensive plan. The Chinese Government Scholarship changes the maths entirely: it pays a monthly stipend of ¥2,500 for undergraduates, ¥3,000 for master's students and ¥3,500 for PhD students, covers tuition and accommodation separately, and that stipend alone covers Tier 2 living costs in full.

How to keep costs down

Take the dormitory bed, eat at the canteen, and pick a Tier 2 city: Chengdu or Xi'an costs half what Shanghai does for the same life. Use the campus meal card (饭卡) for every meal you can: subsidised canteen meals at ¥4–¥12 are the single biggest money-saving tool in China. Walk or cycle on campus, buy a second-hand bicycle, and use the metro instead of ride-hailing. Apply for the CSC scholarship before you worry about budgets: a full CSC award makes China the cheapest destination on this list by a wide margin. Finally, run your own numbers with the study-abroad cost estimator, using your university's actual costs instead of national averages.

Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need per month to live in China as a student?

Most international students spend ¥2,000–¥6,000 a month, excluding tuition. Tier 2 cities sit near ¥2,000–¥3,500; Beijing and Shanghai sit near ¥3,500–¥6,000.

Is ¥2,500 a month enough in China?

In a Tier 2 city with a dormitory bed, yes: the CSC undergraduate stipend is exactly ¥2,500 and covers Tier 2 costs in full. In Beijing or Shanghai it is tight.

What is the cheapest student city in China?

Xi'an, Harbin and Kunming are cheapest, at about ¥1,800–¥3,000 a month, because dormitories there cost ¥300–¥700 and food is cheap.

Does the CSC scholarship cover living costs in China?

Yes. CSC pays ¥2,500 a month for undergraduates, ¥3,000 for master's and ¥3,500 for PhD students, and covers tuition and accommodation separately, so the stipend goes entirely to food, transport and personal costs.

How much is food in China for a student?

Very little by world standards: canteen meals cost ¥6–¥15, and a canteen-based month runs ¥400–¥900. Cooking at home costs about ¥800–¥1,200 a month.
