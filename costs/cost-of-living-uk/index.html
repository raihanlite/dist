Cost of Living in the UK for Bangladeshi Students

24 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · Last checked 24 Nov 2026

The UK is the most expensive of the cheap-to-mid-range destinations, and the gap is almost entirely London. A student outside London lives on about £1,200 a month; the same life in London costs about £1,770. This guide breaks down the real monthly cost of living in the UK for international students: rent, food, transport, bills and the one-off costs of arriving. It is part of the cost of living abroad hub, and the figures here feed the full comparison across eight countries.

Accommodation

Rent decides most of your UK budget. University halls often include bills: at UCL, halls for 2026/27 start at about £154 a week, and premium rooms go well past £530 a week. In a shared house outside London, a room typically costs £500–£700 a month, and the national average rent paid by students is about £575 a month. London students pay about £793 a month on average for accommodation. Expect a deposit of up to five weeks' rent, and since 1 May 2026 a landlord in England cannot ask for rent before you sign, or more than one month in advance afterwards. For the full picture on universities, visas and work rules, read the UK country guide and the UK student visa guide.

Food

Food is the easiest cost to control. The average student spends about £155 a month on groceries, roughly £36 a week, plus about £45 on takeaways and eating out. Seven basics (milk, bread, rice, eggs, chicken, potatoes and onions) cost about £16.60 in total, roughly the price of one cheap restaurant meal. Shopping at ALDI, Lidl or ASDA, buying store brands, and cooking in bulk with flatmates keeps this line near the low end. Halal meat, rice, lentils and spices are easy to find in most UK university cities, and South Asian grocery shops often beat supermarket prices on those staples.

Transport

The average student spends about £73 a month on transport. A 16-25 Railcard costs £35 a year and takes a third off most rail fares, and an 18+ Student Oyster photocard takes 30% off London Travelcards and Bus & Tram Passes. Many university cities are walkable or bike-friendly, which can cut this cost to almost nothing. In London, daily travel costs more, so budget at the top of the range there. One-off arrival costs

Budget for these before you fly. A rental deposit (up to five weeks' rent, about £650–£1,100), the flight from Dhaka, warm clothing (£400–£450 for a winter wardrobe) and bedding and kitchen basics (£60–£120) add roughly £1,500–£2,500 on top of your first month's living costs. Your first month realistically costs about £1,800 outside London and £2,750 in London once the deposit and setup are included. The visa itself costs £558, and the Immigration Health Surcharge is £776 a year, paid upfront for the whole visa. The maintenance money you show for the visa (£1,570 a month in London, £1,203 outside, from 30 Nov 2026) is a minimum to prove, not a budget: real spending in London runs above it.

How to keep costs down

Live outside London if you can: the same degree in Leeds or Sheffield costs hundreds less a month than in London, mostly on rent. Share a house, cook at home, and buy a 16-25 Railcard before your first train trip. Students can work up to 20 hours a week during term time, which typically covers food and transport. Choose halls for the first year if you want bills included and no deposit surprises, then move into a shared house with friends you trust. Finally, run your own numbers with the study-abroad cost estimator, using your university's actual costs instead of national averages.

Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need per month to live in the UK as a student?

Most international students spend £1,200–£1,800 a month, excluding tuition. Outside London, £1,200–£1,400 is realistic; in London, plan for about £1,770.

Is £1,200 a month enough in the UK?

Outside London, yes, if you share a house and cook at home. In London it is not enough: rent alone averages about £793 a month there.

How much is the UK student visa maintenance requirement?

For applications from 30 Nov 2026, £1,570 a month in London and £1,203 outside London, for up to 9 months. That is the amount you must show, not necessarily what you will spend.

What is the cheapest student city in the UK?

Smaller university cities such as Sheffield, Leeds and Newcastle are cheapest, at about £1,150–£1,300 a month, because shared-house rents there start near £450.

Can part-time work cover my living costs in the UK?

Partly. Up to 20 hours a week during term time typically covers food and transport. Rent needs savings, family support or a scholarship.
